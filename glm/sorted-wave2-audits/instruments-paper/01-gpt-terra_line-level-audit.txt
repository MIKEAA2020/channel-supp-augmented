## A. Clear internal inconsistencies

### 1. Table~\ref{tab:summary} caption incorrectly says the envelopes coincide at \(r=0\)

The caption states:

> “The lower and upper envelopes coincide at \(r=0\), in the collapse case, and from \(r=D_{A,B}^{(n)}\) on…”

This is false in general. At \(r=0\),
\[
\beta_0^{\mathrm{best},(n)}(A,B)=1,
\qquad
U_0^{(n)}(A,B)=R_n=2\Bigl(1-\frac1{nd_Bs}\Bigr).
\]
These coincide only when \(nd_Bs=2\), i.e. in the collapse case.

This contradicts:

- the constant-code row of the same table, which correctly lists lower \(1\) and upper \(R_n\);
- Proposition~\ref{prop:gap}, item 1, which explicitly gives the nonzero gap
  \[
  R_n-1=1-\rho_n;
  \]
- Example~\ref{ex:222}, where at \(r=0\) the lower bound is \(1\) and the upper/exact width is \(1.75\);
- Figure~\ref{fig:envelope}(a), which visibly plots different values at \(r=0\).

Suggested replacement:

> “The exact width is known at \(r=0\), in the collapse case, and from \(r=D_{A,B}^{(n)}\) on. The displayed lower and upper envelopes coincide at \(r=0\) only in the collapse case.”

### 2. The conclusion overclaims exact subcritical widths for \(d_A=1\)

The conclusion says:

> “for \(d_A=1\) the subcritical widths are exact.”

That is not established by the preceding results.

What is proved for \(d_A=1\) is:

- the full body equals the replacement body;
- the antipodal profile is exact;
- the top-subcritical width at
  \[
  r=D_{1,B}^{(n)}-1=nd_B^2-2
  \]
  is exactly \(1\), by Corollary~\ref{cor:top-subcritical};
- the constant-code width at \(r=0\) is exactly
  \[
  2\left(1-\frac1{nd_B}\right);
  \]
- the full width function is exact in the collapse case \(nd_B=2\).

But for general \(d_A=1\), \(nd_B>2\), and
\[
0<r<D_{1,B}^{(n)}-1,
\]
the manuscript gives only
\[
1\le \delta_{r,\mathrm{inst}}^\diamond\le U_r^{(n)},
\]
not an exact formula. In fact the constant-code value is generally \(>1\), so “all subcritical widths are exact” cannot be inferred from the exact profile.

Suggested replacement:

> “For \(d_A=1\), the full body is the replacement-state body, so the antipodal profile is exact and the top-subcritical width is exactly \(1\). Apart from the constant-code and collapse cases, the present bounds do not determine every subcritical width.”

This also aligns with Remark~\ref{rem:exact-vs-certified}, Proposition~\ref{prop:gap}, and Table~\ref{tab:summary}.

## B. Undefined or prematurely used notation

### 3. \(\rho_n\) and \(R_n\) are used before they are defined

In Remark~\ref{rem:radius-terminology} and Figure~\ref{fig:radius-notions}, the text uses \(\rho_n\) and \(R_n\), for example:

> “centred in-radius \(\rho_n\)”  
> “covering radius \(R_n\)”

But:

- \(\rho_n\) is only formally introduced later in Definition~\ref{def:beta-best};
- \(R_n\) is only introduced much later in Proposition~\ref{prop:gap}.

This is particularly awkward because the figure appears before both definitions.

Suggested fix: introduce the shorthand immediately after Corollary~\ref{cor:complementarity}, e.g.
\[
s:=\min\{d_A,d_B\},\qquad
\rho_n:=\frac{2}{nd_Bs},\qquad
R_n:=2\Bigl(1-\frac1{nd_Bs}\Bigr).
\]
Then use those symbols consistently thereafter.

## C. Statements that are literally inaccurate, though the underlying proofs can be repaired

### 4. Proposition~\ref{prop:diameter}: the maximally entangled test output is not generally pure

The proof says:

> “Testing the difference of the two replacement instruments on a maximally entangled input produces the difference of two orthogonal flagged pure states…”

For a replacement channel tested on a maximally entangled input, the output is generally
\[
\frac{I_{A_0}}{d_A}\otimes \tau,
\]
not a pure state when \(d_A>1\). The two outputs are orthogonal in their flagged-output factor, and their trace-norm difference is still \(2\), but they are not “two orthogonal flagged pure states” on the full reference-output system.

Suggested repair:

> “Testing on any product pure input produces two orthogonal flagged pure output states. Equivalently, testing on a maximally entangled input produces two orthogonally supported states of trace distance \(2\).”

The diameter conclusion remains correct.

---

### 5. Lemma~\ref{lem:spectral}: “\(B\)-marginal” is the wrong marginal

The proof states:

> “Write \(W\subseteq A_0\) for the support of the \(B\)-marginal \(\Tr_B|v\rangle\langle v|\)…”

But \(\Tr_B|v\rangle\langle v|\) is the marginal on \(A_0\), not the \(B\)-marginal.

Suggested replacement:

> “Write \(W\subseteq A_0\) for the support of the \(A_0\)-marginal \(\Tr_B|v\rangle\langle v|\).”

The subsequent Schmidt-rank argument is correct.

---

### 6. The \(n=1\) observable construction should explicitly choose pure output states

Theorem~\ref{thm:observable} says:

> “choose two orthogonal output states \(\sigma_+,\sigma_-\)”

But Corollary~\ref{cor:join-dimension} subsequently writes

> “where \(\sigma_\pm=|\varphi_\pm\rangle\langle\varphi_\pm|\) are the two orthogonal output states…”

The corollary needs the states to be pure in order to identify
\[
W_{\mathrm{obs}}=\operatorname{span}\{|\varphi_+\rangle,|\varphi_-\rangle\}
\]
as a two-dimensional output support.

Suggested repair in Theorem~\ref{thm:observable}:

> “choose two orthogonal pure output states \(\sigma_\pm=|\varphi_\pm\rangle\langle\varphi_\pm|\).”

This is always possible when \(d_B\ge2\).

---

### 7. Proposition~\ref{prop:join-inst}: endpoint cases are not fully handled in the written proof

The proof treats \(\lambda=0\), then says:

> “Otherwise set \(\vD_1=\dots\) and \(\vD_2=\dots\)”

But when \(\lambda=1\), \(y_2=0\), so \(v=y_2/\|y_2\|_2\) is undefined and \(\vD_2\) cannot be introduced.

The result is correct, but the proof should split into three cases:

- \(\lambda=0\): replacement branch only;
- \(\lambda=1\): \(h\)-branch only;
- \(0<\lambda<1\): use both branches and additivity on orthogonal supports.

---

### 8. The no-programming proof overstates what was shown about \(\operatorname{supp}\pi_U\)

The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:no-programming} establishes that every positive-weight eigenvector in a spectral decomposition of \(\pi_U\) programs \(\mathcal U_U\). It then says:

> “Hence every pure vector in the support of \(\pi_U\) programs \(\mathcal U_U\) exactly.”

That stronger conclusion does not follow immediately from the displayed spectral-decomposition argument, because arbitrary superpositions of eigenvectors introduce program coherences.

It is true with an extra argument: if \(\pi_U\) is full rank on its support, then every pure state \(|v\rangle\) in the support can be included with positive weight in a convex decomposition
\[
\pi_U=\varepsilon |v\rangle\langle v|+(1-\varepsilon)\sigma.
\]
Extremality of the unitary channel then implies that \(|v\rangle\) programs the same unitary. But this argument is absent.

Fortunately, the stronger claim is unnecessary. It suffices to say:

> “Hence every positive-weight eigenvector in a spectral decomposition of \(\pi_U\) programs \(\mathcal U_U\) exactly.”

Applying pure-state no-programming to the eigenvectors of \(\pi_U\) and \(\pi_V\) already proves
\[
\supp \pi_U\perp\supp\pi_V.
\]

## D. Overstatements or unsupported scope claims

### 9. Remark~\ref{rem:barrier} overstates what has been ruled out for joins

The remark says:

> “Orthogonal joins of full-body spheres. These are precisely the constructions already absorbed into \(\beta_r^{\mathrm{best},(n)}\) … and cannot extend the regime of antipodal separation \(2\) beyond it.”

As written, this sounds like a general impossibility statement about all orthogonal-join constructions. The manuscript proves only:

- the replacement-state join;
- the specific observable–replacement instrument-level join;
- the resulting certified value of \(L_{A,B}^{(n)}\).

It does not prove that every conceivable orthogonally supported antipodal construction has already been exhausted. Such a claim also sits uneasily with the repeatedly stated open problem of determining the largest \(r\) for which \(\alpha_r=1\).

Suggested scoped version:

> “The particular replacement and observable–replacement join constructions developed here are already incorporated into \(\beta_r^{\mathrm{best},(n)}\). They do not extend the certified separation-\(2\) regime beyond \(L_{A,B}^{(n)}\).”

---

### 10. The \(d_A=1\) specialisation incorrectly calls the replacement structure “lower-dimensional”

The specialisation says:

> “the antipodal profile … is governed by the lower-dimensional replacement structure of the body.”

For \(d_A=1\),
\[
\Inst_n(\mathbb C,B)=\RepInst_n(\mathbb C,B),
\]
so the replacement body is the entire body and has the same affine dimension
\[
nd_B^2-1.
\]
It is not lower-dimensional in that case.

Suggested replacement:

> “the antipodal profile is governed by the replacement-state realisation of the full body.”

## E. Notational and rigor clarifications worth making

### 11. The output-pinching norm expression is type-inconsistent

In Theorem~\ref{thm:pinching}, the proof writes
\[
\|\mathcal I-(\mathcal P_B\otimes\id_{C_n})\circ\mathcal I\|_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}.
\]
But \(\mathcal P_B\otimes\id_{C_n}\) composes naturally with the flagged channel \(\widehat{\mathcal I}\), not directly with the tuple \(\mathcal I\), unless a componentwise convention is explicitly declared.

Use either
\[
\bigl\|\widehat{\mathcal I}
-(\mathcal P_B\otimes\id_{C_n})\circ\widehat{\mathcal I}\bigr\|_\diamond,
\]
or define \(\widetilde{\mathcal I}_k=\mathcal P_B\circ\mathcal I_k\) and write
\[
\|\mathcal I-\widetilde{\mathcal I}\|_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}.
\]

The estimate itself is correct, using tensor stability:
\[
\|(\id_B-\mathcal P_B)\otimes\id_{C_n}\|_\diamond
=
\|\id_B-\mathcal P_B\|_\diamond.
\]

---

### 12. Lemma~\ref{lem:domination} should clarify its vectorisation/transposition convention

The proof writes
\[
Z=\sum_\alpha |K_\alpha\rangle\!\rangle\langle\!\langle K_\alpha|,
\qquad
\sum_\alpha K_\alpha^\dagger K_\alpha=I_{E_0},
\]
using
\[
|K\rangle\!\rangle=\sum_i|\overline i\rangle\otimes K|i\rangle.
\]

With the displayed conjugate-basis vectorisation, \(\Tr_B|K\rangle\!\rangle\langle\!\langle K|\) is conventionally the transpose/conjugate of \(K^\dagger K\) when expressed in the unbarred coordinate basis. This is harmless because the proof only needs the channel normalization in the chosen identification, but the convention should be stated carefully. Otherwise a reader may see an apparent missing transpose.

For example:

> “Under the fixed conjugate-basis identification of \(E_0\) with \(\overline E\), the condition \(\Tr_B Z=I_{E_0}\) is equivalently \(\sum_\alpha K_\alpha^\dagger K_\alpha=I\).”

---

### 13. The concavity proof should mention attainment, or use \(\varepsilon\)-radii

Lemma~\ref{lem:inradius-concave} writes:

> “Assume \(\mathcal I_i+B_{\mathfrak V}(\rho_i)\subseteq\Inst_n(A,B)\) … Optimising \(\rho_0,\rho_1\) independently gives the concavity.”

Since the in-radius was defined as a supremum, this implicitly assumes the supremum is attained. It is true here, because the body is compact/closed and the direction ball is compact in finite dimension, but that should be stated. Alternatively, use \(\rho_i<\rho_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}(\mathcal I_i)\) and take a limit.

A short repair:

> “For \(\varepsilon>0\), apply the argument with \(\rho_i=\rho_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}(\mathcal I_i)-\varepsilon\), then let \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\).”

---

### 14. “All state-space results transfer” is too broad

In the \(d_A=1\) specialisation, the sentence

> “Hence all state-space results transfer…”

is vague and potentially misleading, especially given that the manuscript does not establish exact widths in every subcritical dimension. It would be safer to specify exactly what transfers:

- the isometric identification;
- the replacement-body antipodal profile;
- the centred/global radii;
- the constant-code result;
- the zero-error threshold;
- any separately proved state-body width statements.

## F. Minor presentation points

### 15. The Figure~\ref{fig:radius-notions} sketch is potentially misleading

The left drawing labels a red dashed circle as the “covering radius \(R_n\),” but it is visibly drawn with a radius comparable to the body diameter, not to the blue body. The caption says schematic, which helps, but it may still visually suggest a generic relation between a centrally inscribed ball, circumscribed ball, and diameter. Since the exact identity
\[
\rho_n+R_n=2
\]
is highly special, consider making the red circle visibly centered at \(\mathcal I_*\) and clearly larger than the body, or label it only schematically without suggesting scale.

### 16. “The condition \(nd_B>1\) yields exactly two cases” is correct but could be clearer

In Theorem~\ref{thm:exact-radius}, write explicitly:

- if \(d_B>1\), use the isometry witness;
- if \(d_B=1\), then \(n\ge2\), use the POVM witness.

This is what the proof does; the current wording is fine but slightly abrupt.

### 17. The phrase “full-direction sphere of Ref.~\cite{CompanionChannels}” should be used consistently

There are several “restricts to” or “reduces to” references to the companion work. Since the present paper uses a different flagged setting and normalised Choi convention, it would help to state once whether the companion paper uses exactly the same Choi normalization and diamond-norm conventions, or whether the formulas have been converted.

## Bottom line
The most important corrections are:

1. fix the false claim that lower and upper envelopes coincide at \(r=0\);
2. retract or qualify the conclusion that all \(d_A=1\) subcritical widths are exact;
3. define \(\rho_n\) and \(R_n\) before their first use;
4. repair the few literal proof inaccuracies and scope overclaims listed above.
