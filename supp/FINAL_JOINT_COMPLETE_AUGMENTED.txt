\begin{abstract}
We determine the exact affine and metric parameters of the compact convex body of $n$-outcome quantum instruments between finite-dimensional quantum systems. Its affine dimension is
\[
D_{A,B}^{(n)}=d_A^2(nd_B^2-1).
\]
Assume $n d_B>1$, so $\Inst_n(A,B)$ is not a singleton; when $n=d_B=1$ the body is the single trace functional, $D_{A,B}^{(1)}=0$, its direction space is $\{0\}$ and $\rho_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}=+\infty$ by the supremum convention while $R_{\mathrm{inst}}=0$.
Around the uniform depolarising instrument $\mathcal I_*$, for $n d_B>1$ the centred relative diamond radius is exactly
\[
\rho_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}(\mathcal I_*)
=\frac{2}{n d_B\min\{d_A,d_B\}},
\]
and its covering radius is exactly
\[
R_{\mathrm{inst}}(\mathcal I_*)
=2\left(1-\frac{1}{n d_B\min\{d_A,d_B\}}\right),
\]
with $R_{\mathrm{inst}}=0$ in the singleton case $n=d_B=1$.
Flagged replacement instruments contain a continuous image of $S^{n d_B^2-2}$ with antipodal instrument-diamond distance two when $n d_B>1$, so
\[
\alpha_r(\Inst_n(A,B))=1
\qquad(0\le r\le n d_B^2-2).
\]
For intermediate latent dimensions $n d_B^2-1\le r< D_{A,B}^{(n)}$ (empty when $d_A=1$, where $D_{1,B}^{(n)}=n d_B^2-1$), the centred ball gives
\[
\alpha_r(\Inst_n(A,B))
\ge \frac{2}{n d_B\min\{d_A,d_B\}},
\]
and $\alpha_r=0$ for $r\ge D_{A,B}^{(n)}$ by Borsuk--Ulam.
These two certificates combine into a certified lower envelope $\beta_r^{(n)}(A,B)$. We also give constructive upper bounds from convex projection and balanced input/output pinching, producing an explicit bracket
\[
\beta_r^{(n)}(A,B)
\le
\delta_{r,\mathrm{inst}}^\diamond(A,B)
\le
U_r^{(n)}(A,B).
\]
The zero-error continuous-encoding threshold is the affine dimension $D_{A,B}^{(n)}$. The single-outcome case recovers the channel results, and the one-dimensional output case gives finite-outcome measurements. This separates representational dimension from programmable processors and makes the $1/n$ suppression of the centred radius versus the $n$-fold growth of the affine dimension explicit.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Quantum channels are the basic objects of continuous compression. A natural generalisation is a quantum instrument: a collection of completely positive, trace-nonincreasing maps whose sum is a channel. Instruments describe both the probability of an outcome and the conditional post-measurement state, and they are the appropriate model for finite-outcome measurements and adaptive protocols.

The main question is the same as for channels: can the compact convex body of $n$-outcome instruments be encoded continuously into a low-dimensional latent space and decoded with uniformly small diamond-norm error? The answer has two regimes. At small latent dimension, input-independent preparation of a flagged output state already forces error one. At larger dimensions, the full input-dependent instrument body imposes a ball bound that scales as $1/n$.

A separate physical question is whether an abstract code can be realised by a fixed programmable processor. For unitary channels, the no-programming theorem and a compactness argument show that exact finite-dimensional evaluation is impossible, and vanishing error forces unbounded program dimension. This is stated as motivation and summarised in Appendix~\ref{app:no-programming}; the main contribution of this paper is the exact geometry of instruments and its compression consequences.

\section{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:prelim}

Throughout, $A$ and $B$ are finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces of dimensions $d_A$ and $d_B$, and $n\in\mathbb{N}$, $n\ge1$ is the number of outcomes. Let $A_0\simeq A$ be a reference copy and fix conjugate bases in $A_0$ and $A$. Define the maximally entangled state
\[
|\omega_A\rangle
=\frac1{\sqrt{d_A}}\sum_{j=1}^{d_A}|j\rangle_{A_0}|j\rangle_A.
\]
For a map $\Phi:\Lin(A)\to\Lin(B)$, the normalised Choi operator is
\[
\widehat J_\Phi=(\id_{A_0}\otimes\Phi)(|\omega_A\rangle\langle\omega_A|).
\]
The completely depolarising channel is
\[
\Omega_{A,B}(X)=\Tr(X)\frac{I_B}{d_B},
\qquad
\widehat J_{\Omega_{A,B}}
=\frac{I_{A_0\otimes B}}{d_Ad_B}.
\]

Let $C_n$ be an $n$-dimensional classical flag space with basis $|1\rangle,\ldots,|n\rangle$. Quantum instruments describe both an outcome probability and the corresponding conditional state transformation \cite{DaviesLewis1970,Ozawa1984}; finite-dimensional realisation schemes are given in~\cite{ChiribellaEtAl2009Instruments}.

\begin{definition}[Instrument, flagged direction, and norm]
An $n$-outcome instrument from $A$ to $B$ is a tuple
$\mathcal I=(\mathcal I_1,\ldots,\mathcal I_n)$ of completely positive,
trace-nonincreasing maps whose sum is a channel. Write
$\Inst_n(A,B)$ for the set of all such instruments. The flagged channel of
$\mathcal I$ is
\[
\widehat{\mathcal I}(X)
=\sum_{k=1}^n\mathcal I_k(X)\otimes|k\rangle\langle k|.
\]
For a tuple $\vec\Delta=(\Delta_1,\ldots,\Delta_n)$ of
Hermiticity-preserving maps, define the flagged map and instrument diamond
norm by
\[
\widehat{\vec\Delta}(X)
=\sum_{k=1}^n\Delta_k(X)\otimes|k\rangle\langle k|,
\qquad
\|\vec\Delta\|_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}
=\|\widehat{\vec\Delta}\|_\diamond.
\]
Equivalently, block diagonality gives the operational formula
\[
\|\vec\Delta\|_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}
=\sup_{\rho\in\Sstate(A_0\otimes A)}
\sum_{k=1}^n
\| (\id_{A_0}\otimes\Delta_k)(\rho)\|_1.
\]
A reference system of input dimension $d_A$ suffices for the diamond norm
\cite{Watrous2018}. The summands are the unconditional, generally
subnormalised outcome contributions; no worst conditional-branch
normalisation is used.
\end{definition}

The ambient affine space and its direction space are, respectively,
\begin{align*}
\mathfrak A_{A,B}^{(n)}
&=\left\{(\Phi_1,\ldots,\Phi_n):
\Phi_k\text{ Hermiticity-preserving},\quad
\sum_{k=1}^n\Tr_B\widehat J_{\Phi_k}
=\frac{I_{A_0}}{d_A}\right\},\\
\mathfrak V_{A,B}^{(n)}
&=\left\{(\Delta_1,\ldots,\Delta_n):
\Delta_k\text{ Hermiticity-preserving},\quad
\sum_{k=1}^n\Tr_B\widehat J_{\Delta_k}=0\right\}.
\end{align*}
Thus only the summed direction is trace-annihilating; individual outcome
components need not be. For $\mathcal I_0\in\mathfrak A_{A,B}^{(n)}$,
define the closed relative instrument-diamond ball
\[
\overline B_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}^{\mathrm{rel}}
(\mathcal I_0,\rho)
=\left\{\mathcal I_0+\vec\Delta:
\vec\Delta\in\mathfrak V_{A,B}^{(n)},\quad
\|\vec\Delta\|_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}\leq\rho\right\}.
\]
For $\mathcal I_0\in\Inst_n(A,B)$, its centred relative in-radius is
\[
\rho_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}(\mathcal I_0)
=\sup\left\{\rho\geq0:
\overline B_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}^{\mathrm{rel}}
(\mathcal I_0,\rho)\subseteq\Inst_n(A,B)\right\}.
\]

Let $\mathcal I_*$ be the uniform depolarising instrument with
$(\mathcal I_*)_k=\Omega_{A,B}/n$.

\begin{remark}[Operational meaning]
For instruments, $\|\vec\Delta\|_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}=\sup_{\rho}\sum_k\|(\id\otimes\Delta_k)(\rho)\|_1$ equals the optimal bias in discriminating two instruments with a single use and arbitrary reference assistance, summed over unconditional outcome contributions. Hence every radius and width has direct single-shot discrimination meaning.
\end{remark}

\section{Exact affine dimension and centred radius}
\label{sec:affine-radius}

\begin{proposition}[Affine dimension of the instrument body]
\label{prop:instrument-dimension}
The affine dimension of the $n$-outcome instrument body is
\[
D_{A,B}^{(n)}=d_A^2(nd_B^2-1).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
An instrument Choi tuple contains $n$ Hermitian operators on $A_0\otimes B$,
with total real dimension $n d_A^2d_B^2$. The normalisation constraint
\[
\sum_{k=1}^n\Tr_B\widehat J_{\mathcal I_k}=\frac{I_{A_0}}{d_A}
\]
imposes $d_A^2$ independent real affine equations; $\dim_{\mathbb R}\Herm(A_0)=d_A^2$.
The tuple with every component equal to $\Omega_{A,B}/n$, i.e., $\widehat J_{(\mathcal I_*)_k}=I_{A_0\otimes B}/(n d_Ad_B)$, has positive-definite
blocks. Since every Choi block of $\mathcal I_*$ is positive definite, sufficiently small perturbations in every direction of $\mathfrak V_{A,B}^{(n)}$ remain block-positive; hence $\mathcal I_*$ is a relative interior point and the affine hull equals $\mathfrak A_{A,B}^{(n)}$, so positivity introduces no further affine equalities.
The linear map $(X_1,\dots,X_n)\mapsto\sum_k\Tr_B X_k$ from $\bigoplus_k\Herm(A_0\otimes B)$ to $\Herm(A_0)$ is surjective: for $H\in\Herm(A_0)$, set $X_1=H\otimes I_B/d_B$ (since $\Tr_B(H\otimes I_B/d_B)=H$) and $X_{k\ge2}=0$. Hence the $d_A^2$ affine constraints are independent and the affine count $n d_A^2 d_B^2-d_A^2=d_A^2(nd_B^2-1)$ is attained.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Instrument spectral bound]
\label{lem:instrument-spectral}
Let $s=\min\{d_A,d_B\}$ and let
$\vec\Delta\in\mathfrak V_{A,B}^{(n)}$. If $X$ is the normalised Choi
operator of its flagged map $\widehat{\vec\Delta}$ (i.e. $X=\widehat J_{\widehat{\vec\Delta}}$ with $\widehat J$ extended to maps $A\to B\otimes C_n$ via the same normalised convention), then
\[
\max\{0,\lambda_{\max}(-X)\}
\leq\frac{s}{2d_A}
\|\vec\Delta\|_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}.
\]
In particular $\lambda_{\max}(-X)\le s\|\vec\Delta\|_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}/2d_A$ when $\lambda_{\max}(-X)>0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $\lambda_{\max}(-X)\le0$ the inequality is immediate because the right-hand side is non-negative. Assume $\lambda_{\max}(-X)>0$.
Write
\[
X=\sum_{k=1}^nX_k\otimes|k\rangle\langle k|,
\qquad X_k=\widehat J_{\Delta_k}.
\]
Since $X$ is block-diagonal, $\lambda_{\max}(-X)=\max_k\lambda_{\max}(-X_k)$. Choose an outcome $k_0$ attaining the maximum and a unit vector
$|v\rangle\in A_0\otimes B$ such that
\[
X_{k_0}|v\rangle=-\lambda|v\rangle,
\qquad
\lambda=\lambda_{\max}(-X).
\]
Let $S\subseteq A_0$ be the support of
$\Tr_B|v\rangle\langle v|$ and put $b=\dim S\leq s$. If
$|e_1\rangle,\ldots,|e_b\rangle$ is an orthonormal basis of $S$, let
$|\overline e_j\rangle\in A$ denote its coordinatewise conjugate under the
fixed Choi bases. The normalised maximally entangled state on the
basis-conjugate input subspace is
\[
|\omega_S\rangle
=\frac1{\sqrt b}\sum_{j=1}^b
|e_j\rangle_{A_0}|\overline e_j\rangle_A.
\]
With $P_S$ the projection onto $S$, the normalised-Choi convention gives
\begin{align*}
Y&=(\id_{A_0}\otimes\widehat{\vec\Delta})
(|\omega_S\rangle\langle\omega_S|)\\
&=\frac{d_A}{b}
(P_S\otimes I_{B\otimes C_n})X
(P_S\otimes I_{B\otimes C_n}).
\end{align*}
The direction constraint implies
\[
\Tr Y
=\frac{d_A}{b}\Tr\left[
P_S\sum_{k=1}^n\Tr_BX_k\right]=0,
\]
so $Y$ is Hermitian and traceless. Since
$|v\rangle\in S\otimes B$, testing on
$|v\rangle\otimes|k_0\rangle$ gives
\[
\langle v,k_0|Y|v,k_0\rangle=-\frac{d_A}{b}\lambda.
\]
For a traceless Hermitian operator $Y=Y_+-Y_-$,
\[
\Tr Y_+=\Tr Y_-=\frac12\|Y\|_1
\]
and
$\langle w|Y|w\rangle\geq-\Tr Y_-$ for every unit vector $|w\rangle$.
Consequently,
\[
\|Y\|_1
\geq-2\langle v,k_0|Y|v,k_0\rangle
=\frac{2d_A}{b}\lambda
\geq\frac{2d_A}{s}\lambda.
\]
The state $|\omega_S\rangle\langle\omega_S|$ is admissible in the
instrument diamond-norm optimisation, so
$\|\vec\Delta\|_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}\geq\|Y\|_1$. Rearranging proves
the result.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Instrument diamond ball]
\label{thm:instrument-ball}
Let $s=\min\{d_A,d_B\}$. Then
\[
\overline B_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}^{\mathrm{rel}}
\left(\mathcal I_*,\frac{2}{n d_Bs}\right)
\subseteq\Inst_n(A,B).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The normalised Choi operator of the flagged channel associated with
$\mathcal I_*$ is block diagonal in the classical flag, and every one of its
eigenvalues equals $1/(n d_Ad_B)$. If
$\vec\Delta\in\mathfrak V_{A,B}^{(n)}$ and
$\|\vec\Delta\|_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}
\leq2/(n d_Bs)$, Lemma~\ref{lem:instrument-spectral} gives
\[
\lambda_{\max}(-X)\le\frac1{n d_Ad_B},
\]
where $X=\widehat J_{\widehat{\vec\Delta}}$ is as in Lemma~\ref{lem:instrument-spectral}.
The full flagged Choi operator therefore remains positive. Since it is
block diagonal, this is equivalent to positivity of every outcome block.
The summed marginal is unchanged by the direction constraint, so the
perturbed tuple is an instrument.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact centred instrument radius]
\label{thm:instrument-exact}
Assume $n d_B>1$. The centred relative instrument-diamond in-radius about
$\mathcal I_*$ is exactly
\[
\rho_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}(\mathcal I_*)
=\frac{2}{n d_B\min\{d_A,d_B\}}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put $s=\min\{d_A,d_B\}$. Theorem~\ref{thm:instrument-ball} gives the lower
bound. For the reverse inequality, it suffices to construct a unit-norm
direction reaching the boundary at the stated distance.

The condition $n d_B>1$ yields exactly two cases: (i) $d_B>1$, any $n\ge1$; or (ii) $d_B=1$, $n\ge2$. The proof follows those branches. First suppose $d_B>1$. Choose an $s$-dimensional input subspace
$S_A\subseteq A$. There exist two isometries $V_0,V_1:S_A\to B$ with
\[
\Tr(V_0^\dagger V_1)=0.
\]
Indeed, if $s=1$, choose two orthogonal output unit vectors; if $s\geq2$,
fix $V_0$, choose a trace-zero unitary $U$ on $S_A$, and set $V_1=V_0U$.

Let $P_{S_A}$ be the input projection and define
\[
\mathcal E_j(Z)=V_jP_{S_A}ZP_{S_A}V_j^\dagger,
\qquad j=0,1.
\]
Each map is completely positive and trace-nonincreasing, hence has diamond
norm at most one. Choose an orthonormal basis
$|e_1\rangle,\ldots,|e_s\rangle$ of $S_A$ and set
\[
|\omega_{S_A}\rangle
=\frac1{\sqrt s}\sum_{a=1}^s
|\overline e_a\rangle_{A_0}|e_a\rangle_A,
\qquad
P_j=(\id\otimes\mathcal E_j)
(|\omega_{S_A}\rangle\langle\omega_{S_A}|).
\]
The $P_j$ are rank-one projectors, and
\[
\Tr_BP_0=\Tr_BP_1=\frac{P_{\overline S_A}}s.
\]
Moreover,
\[
\Tr(P_0P_1)
=\left|\frac1s\Tr(V_0^\dagger V_1)\right|^2=0.
\]
Thus the displayed maximally entangled input produces orthogonal pure
outputs, and
\[
\|\mathcal E_1-\mathcal E_0\|_\diamond=2.
\]
The normalised Choi operators on the full input space satisfy
\[
\widehat J_{\mathcal E_j}=\frac{s}{d_A}P_j.
\]

Define the instrument direction
\[
\Delta_1=\frac12(\mathcal E_1-\mathcal E_0),
\qquad
\Delta_k=0\quad(k\geq2).
\]
Since
$\Tr\mathcal E_1(Z)=\Tr(P_{S_A}Z)=\Tr\mathcal E_0(Z)$,
the summed direction is trace-annihilating and
$\vec\Delta\in\mathfrak V_{A,B}^{(n)}$. Its flagged diamond norm is one,
and its first normalised Choi block is
\[
\widehat J_{\Delta_1}=\frac{s}{2d_A}(P_1-P_0).
\]
At
\[
t=\frac{2}{n d_Bs},
\]
the first block of $\mathcal I_*+t\vec\Delta$ equals
\[
\frac1{n d_Ad_B}
\left(I_{A_0\otimes B}+P_1-P_0\right).
\]
This operator is positive, and the unit vector supporting $P_0$ is a zero
eigenvector because $P_0P_1=0$. All remaining blocks are unchanged, and
the marginal constraint is preserved because
$\Tr_BP_0=\Tr_BP_1$. Hence the ray reaches the relative boundary at
distance $t$. For any $t'>t$, the eigenvalue on the support of $P_0$ becomes $\frac1{n d_Ad_B}-t'\frac{s}{2d_A}<0$, so $\mathcal I_*+t'\vec\Delta$ has a negative Choi block and is not an instrument. Therefore no closed relative ball of radius larger than $t$ is contained in $\Inst_n(A,B)$, proving maximality.

It remains to treat $d_B=1$, in which case $n\geq2$ and an instrument is a
POVM. Choose a rank-one projection $P$ on $A$ and define the positive
functional
\[
\mathcal F(Z)=\Tr(PZ).
\]
Set
\[
\Delta_1=-\frac12\mathcal F,
\qquad
\Delta_2=\frac12\mathcal F,
\qquad
\Delta_k=0\quad(k\geq3).
\]
The summed direction vanishes. For every reference-assisted input state
$\xi$, let
\[
\zeta_R
=\Tr_A[(I_R\otimes P)\xi(I_R\otimes P)]
=\Tr_A[(I_R\otimes P)\xi]\geq0.
\]
Then
\[
(\id_R\otimes\widehat{\vec\Delta})(\xi)
=\frac12\zeta_R\otimes
\bigl(|2\rangle\langle2|-|1\rangle\langle1|\bigr),
\]
whose trace norm is $\Tr\zeta_R\leq1$. Equality is attained by an input
supported on $P$, so
$\|\vec\Delta\|_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}=1$. In the fixed Choi basis,
\[
\widehat J_{\mathcal F}=\frac{P^{\mathsf T}}{d_A}.
\]
At $t=2/n$, the first two Choi blocks of
$\mathcal I_*+t\vec\Delta$ are
\[
\frac{I_{A_0}-P^{\mathsf T}}{n d_A},
\qquad
\frac{I_{A_0}+P^{\mathsf T}}{n d_A},
\]
while all other blocks remain $I_{A_0}/(n d_A)$. These blocks are positive,
the first is singular, and their sum is $I_{A_0}/d_A$. Thus this ray reaches
the POVM boundary at distance $2/n$, which is the stated formula when
$d_B=s=1$. For $t'>2/n$, the first block $I_{A_0}/(n d_A)-t'P^{\mathsf T}/(2d_A)$ has eigenvalue $1/(n d_A)-t'/(2d_A)<0$ on the range of $P^{\mathsf T}$, so $\mathcal I_*+t'\vec\Delta$ leaves the POVM body, proving maximality.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Centred vs global in-radius]
Theorem~\ref{thm:instrument-exact} determines the largest ball centred at $\mathcal I_*$. It does not prove that $\mathcal I_*$ maximises the in-radius over all centres in $\Inst_n(A,B)$. The global in-radius $\sup_{\mathcal I_0\in\Inst_n}\rho_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}(\mathcal I_0)$ could be larger than $\rho_n=2/(n d_B s)$; any such larger ball would improve $\alpha_r$ and $\beta_r^{(n)}$ in the intermediate regime $m_n\le r<D_n$. Determining the global in-radius is an open geometric problem.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Dimension specialisations and the degenerate case]
For $n=1$ and $d_B>1$, the instrument direction space and norm reduce to the
ordinary trace-annihilating channel direction space and diamond norm. Hence
Theorem~\ref{thm:instrument-exact} gives
\[
\rho_\diamond(\Omega_{A,B})
=\frac{2}{d_B\min\{d_A,d_B\}},
\]
in agreement with the exact all-dimensional channel theorem in the main
article. If instead $n=d_B=1$, the instrument body consists of the unique
trace functional, its direction space is zero, and every relative ball is
that singleton. Under the supremum definition above, its centred relative
in-radius is therefore $+\infty$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Physical origin of the radius]
Each Choi block of the uniform instrument has positivity margin
$1/(n d_Ad_B)$, while a unit instrument-diamond perturbation can create a
negative Choi eigenvalue of size at most
$s/(2d_A)$, where $s=\min\{d_A,d_B\}$. Their ratio is the exact radius
$2/(n d_Bs)$. The factor $1/n$ reflects the distribution of the uniform
trace budget among $n$ outcomes, whereas $s$ is the largest Schmidt
rank that can be supported simultaneously by the input and quantum output.
For a one-dimensional output, the quantum isometry witness is replaced by an
outcome-transfer direction: a rank-one measurement effect is moved between
two classical outcomes. Thus quantum output support and classical
branching contribute to the same positivity budget in different ways.
\end{remark}

\section{Exact covering radius}
\label{sec:covering}

\begin{lemma}[Bipartite domination for instrument blocks]
\label{lem:instrument-block-domination}
Let $\sigma_{RB}\ge0$ be subnormalised, with
$\rho_R=\Tr_B\sigma_{RB}$ and $r=\operatorname{rank}\rho_R$. Then
\[
\sigma_{RB}
\le\min\{r,d_B\}\,\rho_R\otimes I_B.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The zero operator is immediate. Otherwise, restrict $R$ to the support of
$\rho_R$ (so $\rho_R^{-1/2}$ is defined on that support) and set
\[
Z=(\rho_R^{-1/2}\otimes I_B)\sigma_{RB}
(\rho_R^{-1/2}\otimes I_B).
\]
Then $Z\ge0$ and $\Tr_BZ=I_R$. Write $Z=\sum_\alpha|K_\alpha\rangle\!\rangle\langle\!\langle K_\alpha|$ with $\sum_\alpha K_\alpha^\dagger K_\alpha=I_R$, using the vectorisation convention $|K\rangle\!\rangle=\sum_i|\bar i\rangle\otimes K|i\rangle$. If a unit vector $|v\rangle$ corresponds to a matrix $V:R\to B$ of rank $b$, then $b\le\min\{r,d_B\}$ and
\begin{align*}
\langle v|Z|v\rangle
&=\sum_\alpha|\Tr(V^\dagger K_\alpha)|^2\\
&\le b\sum_\alpha\Tr(VK_\alpha^\dagger K_\alpha V^\dagger)=b,
\end{align*}
where the inequality uses $|\Tr M|^2\le\rank(M)\|M\|_2^2$ with $M=K_\alpha V^\dagger$ of rank at most $b$, i.e. Cauchy--Schwarz on its support. Thus $Z\le\min\{r,d_B\}I$, and conjugation by $\rho_R^{1/2}\otimes I_B$ proves the result.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact instrument covering radius]
\label{thm:instrument-covering}
Let $s=\min\{d_A,d_B\}$. For $\Psi\in\mathfrak A_{A,B}^{(n)}$, define
\[
R_{\mathrm{inst}}(\Psi)=\sup_{\mathcal I\in\Inst_n(A,B)}\|\mathcal I-\Psi\|_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}.
\]
Then the uniform depolarising instrument is an optimal centre and
\[
R_{\mathrm{inst}}(\mathcal I_*)=2\left(1-\frac1{n d_Bs}\right)=2-\rho_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}(\mathcal I_*).
\]
Consequently, the exact constant-code instrument width has this value.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix a reference-assisted input state and write the flagged output of an
instrument as
\[
\sigma_{RBC_n}=\sum_{k=1}^n\sigma_{k,RB}\otimes|k\rangle\langle k|,\qquad\sum_k\Tr_B\sigma_{k,RB}=\rho_R.
\]
Put $\rho_{k,R}=\Tr_B\sigma_{k,RB}$. Lemma~\ref{lem:instrument-block-domination} and $\operatorname{rank}\rho_{k,R}\le d_A$ give
\[
\sigma_{k,RB}\le s\,\rho_{k,R}\otimes I_B\le s\,\rho_R\otimes I_B.
\]
Therefore
\[
\sigma_{RBC_n}\le n d_Bs\left(\rho_R\otimes\frac{I_B}{d_B}\otimes\frac{I_{C_n}}n\right).
\]
The state in parentheses is the output of $\mathcal I_*$. If states $\sigma\le K\tau$, let $P$ be the positive spectral projection of $\sigma-\tau$ and put $a=\Tr(P\sigma)$ and $b=\Tr(P\tau)$. Then $a\le Kb$ and $a\le1$, so
\[
\frac12\|\sigma-\tau\|_1=a-b\le a\left(1-\frac1K\right)\le1-\frac1K.
\]
Taking the supremum over inputs yields $R_{\mathrm{inst}}(\mathcal I_*)\le2(1-1/(n d_Bs))$.

For equality, choose an $s$-dimensional input subspace $S\subseteq A$, an isometry $V:S\to B$, and a state $\omega$ on $B$. With $P_S$ the subspace projection, define $\Phi(X)=VP_SXP_SV^\dagger+\Tr[(I_A-P_S)X]\,\omega$. Set $\mathcal I_1=\Phi$ and $\mathcal I_k=0$ for $k\ge2$. A maximally entangled input of Schmidt rank $s$ on that subspace gives a pure output in the first flag, whereas $\mathcal I_*$ gives the maximally mixed state on the corresponding $n d_Bs$-dimensional support. Their trace distance is $2(1-1/(n d_Bs))$.

Optimality of the centre: postcomposition by output unitaries and permutation of outcome labels preserve the instrument body and its metric. The function $R_{\mathrm{inst}}$ is convex and invariant under these actions. Averaging any $\Psi\in\mathfrak A_{A,B}^{(n)}$ first over output unitaries and then over outcome permutations makes every component $X\mapsto\frac1n\sum_k\frac{\Tr\Psi_k(X)}{d_B}I_B=\frac{\Tr X}{n d_B}I_B$, because the summed map is trace preserving. The averaged centre is therefore $\mathcal I_*$. Jensen's inequality gives $R_{\mathrm{inst}}(\mathcal I_*)\le R_{\mathrm{inst}}(\Psi)$. Moreover $\inf_{\Psi\in\mathfrak A}R_{\mathrm{inst}}(\Psi)=\inf_{\Psi\in\Inst_n}R_{\mathrm{inst}}(\Psi)=R_{\mathrm{inst}}(\mathcal I_*)$, so the constant-code width $\delta_{0,\mathrm{inst}}^\diamond=R_{\mathrm{inst}}(\mathcal I_*)$ is admissible with a physical instrument decoder.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Metric complementarity of the uniform instrument]
\label{cor:metric-complementarity}
For all $n\ge1$ and $d_A,d_B$ with $n d_B>1$,
\[
\rho_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}(\mathcal I_*)+R_{\mathrm{inst}}(\mathcal I_*)=\operatorname{diam}_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}(\Inst_n(A,B))=2.
\]
The uniform depolarising instrument divides the extremal chord joining the boundary point $\mathcal I_*+\rho_n\vec\Delta$ and the extreme pure instrument at distance $R_n$ in ratio $\rho_n:(2-\rho_n)$.
\end{corollary}

\section{Flagged replacement instruments and lower envelope}
\label{sec:replacement}

Let
\[
\Sstate_{\mathrm{flag}}(B\otimes C_n)
=\left\{
\tau=\sum_{k=1}^n\tau_k\otimes|k\rangle\langle k|:
\tau_k\geq0,\quad\sum_{k=1}^n\Tr\tau_k=1
\right\}.
\]
For any instrument $\mathcal I$, its flagged channel $\widehat{\mathcal I}$ is a channel, hence $\|\widehat{\mathcal I}\|_\diamond=1$. Moreover $\|\Phi\otimes\id_{C_n}\|_\diamond=\|\Phi\|_\diamond$ by stability of the diamond norm under tensoring with an identity channel (multiplicativity $\|\Phi\otimes\Psi\|_\diamond=\|\Phi\|_\diamond\|\Psi\|_\diamond$ and $\|\id_{C_n}\|_\diamond=1$; submultiplicativity concerns composition). For $\tau\in\Sstate_{\mathrm{flag}}(B\otimes C_n)$, define the replacement
instrument $\vec{\mathcal R}_\tau$ by
\[
(\vec{\mathcal R}_\tau)_k(X)=\Tr(X)\tau_k.
\]
Each component is completely positive and trace-nonincreasing, since $\Tr\tau_k\le1$ (positivity and $\sum_k\Tr\tau_k=1$ implies each $\Tr\tau_k\in[0,1]$), and their sum
is a channel because $\sum_k\Tr\tau_k=1$. Write
\[
\operatorname{RepInst}_n(A,B)
=\{\vec{\mathcal R}_\tau:
\tau\in\Sstate_{\mathrm{flag}}(B\otimes C_n)\}.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Replacement-instrument isometry]
\label{lem:replacement-instrument}
For all flagged states $\tau$ and $\sigma$,
\[
\|\vec{\mathcal R}_\tau-\vec{\mathcal R}_\sigma
\|_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}
=\|\tau-\sigma\|_1
=\sum_{k=1}^n\|\tau_k-\sigma_k\|_1.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The flagged channel of $\vec{\mathcal R}_\tau$ is the replacement channel
$X\mapsto\Tr(X)\tau$. For every reference-assisted input state $\xi$,
\[
(\id\otimes
(\widehat{\vec{\mathcal R}_\tau}
 -\widehat{\vec{\mathcal R}_\sigma}))(\xi)
=\Tr_A(\xi)\otimes(\tau-\sigma).
\]
The first tensor factor $\Tr_A(\xi)$ is a state with trace one, so for every normalized input $\xi$, $\|\Tr_A(\xi)\otimes(\tau-\sigma)\|_1=\|\tau-\sigma\|_1$ by multiplicativity. Hence the equality holds for every input and the supremum is unnecessary for the first equality. The second follows from block diagonality in the flag.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Flagged-state picture]
A flagged replacement instrument discards the input and prepares a classical--quantum state $\tau=\sum_k\tau_k\otimes|k\rangle\langle k|$ with $\sum_k{\rm Tr}\tau_k=1$; each $\tau_k$ is subnormalized with ${\rm Tr}\tau_k\le1$. The flagged channel is the replacement channel $X\mapsto{\rm Tr}(X)\tau$, so instrument diamond distance equals flagged trace distance $\sum_k\|\tau_k-\sigma_k\|_1$. Orthogonal flagged supports give distance $2$.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Instrument antipodal profile]
For any nonempty metric space $(K,d)$, define generally
\[
\alpha_r(K)=\frac12\sup_{h\in C(S^r,K)}\min_{u\in S^r}d(h(u),h(-u)),
\]
with the instrument diamond norm when $K\subseteq\Inst_n(A,B)$. In particular,
\[
\alpha_r(\Inst_n(A,B))
=\frac12\sup_{h\in C(S^r,\Inst_n(A,B))}
\min_{u\in S^r}
\|h(u)-h(-u)\|_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}.
\]
For compact metric $K\subseteq L$, define $\delta_r^{\mathrm c}(K;L)=\inf_{f:K\to\mathbb R^r\,\mathrm{cont},\,g:\mathbb R^r\to L}\sup_{x\in K}d(g(f(x)),x)$ with the instrument diamond metric when $L=\Inst_n(A,B)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Perfectly separated replacement instruments]
\label{thm:instrument-jordan-sphere}
Assume $n d_B>1$. Flagged replacement instruments contain a continuous
image of $S^{n d_B^2-2}$ whose antipodes have instrument-diamond distance two.
Consequently,
\[
\alpha_r(\Inst_n(A,B))=1,
\qquad
0\leq r\leq n d_B^2-2.
\]
For the replacement-instrument family itself, the complete profile is
\[
\alpha_r(\operatorname{RepInst}_n(A,B))
=\begin{cases}
1,&0\leq r\leq n d_B^2-2,\\
0,&r\geq n d_B^2-1.
\end{cases}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The real vector space of flag-block-diagonal Hermitian operators on
$B\otimes C_n$ has dimension $n d_B^2$. Its trace-zero subspace
\[
\mathfrak H_{\mathrm{flag},0}
=\left\{
X=\sum_{k=1}^nX_k\otimes|k\rangle\langle k|:
X_k\in\Herm(B),\quad\sum_k\Tr X_k=0
\right\}
\]
has dimension $n d_B^2-1$. Choose any Euclidean norm on $\mathfrak H_{\mathrm{flag},0}$ and let its unit sphere $S(\mathfrak H_{\mathrm{flag},0})\cong S^{n d_B^2-2}$ be the sphere. The resulting map $X\mapsto X_+/\Tr X_+$ is a continuous image of a sphere (not claimed to be an embedding), which suffices for antipodal-width arguments.
For every point $X$ on the sphere, write its Jordan decomposition as
$X=X_+-X_-$. Functional calculus preserves block diagonality and makes
$X\mapsto X_+$ continuous. Since $X$ is nonzero and traceless,
\[
t_X:=\Tr X_+=\Tr X_->0.
\]
The map
\[
h(X)=\frac{X_+}{t_X}
\]
is therefore continuous and takes values in
$\Sstate_{\mathrm{flag}}(B\otimes C_n)$. Since
$h(-X)=X_-/t_X$ and the two Jordan supports are orthogonal,
\[
\|h(X)-h(-X)\|_1=2.
\]
Composing $h$ with the replacement-instrument embedding from
Lemma~\ref{lem:replacement-instrument} gives the required sphere of
instruments. Restriction to equatorial spheres supplies every smaller
index. The reverse profile inequality follows because flagged channels,
and hence instruments in the flagged norm, have diameter at most two.

It remains to determine the replacement-family profile above this range.
The flagged state space is a convex body of affine dimension
$n d_B^2-1$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:replacement-instrument} is an isometric
homeomorphism onto $\operatorname{RepInst}_n(A,B)$. Compose any map
$S^r\to\operatorname{RepInst}_n(A,B)$ with the inverse isometry and affine
coordinates in $\mathbb R^{n d_B^2-1}$. For
$r\geq n d_B^2-1$, Borsuk--Ulam produces an antipodal collision. Every
such map therefore has minimum antipodal distance zero, proving the second
part of the profile formula.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Observable sphere beyond replacement]
\label{thm:observable-sphere}
If $n\ge2$ and $d_A\ge2$, let $\Herm_0(A)$ have real dimension $d_A^2-1$ and unit sphere $S^{d_A^2-2}$. For $H\in\Herm_0(A)$, $\|H\|_\infty>0$, set $K(H)=H/\|H\|_\infty$, $E_\pm(H)=(I_A\pm K(H))/2$. Fix $\sigma\in\Sstate(B)$ and define $n$-outcome instrument $\mathcal I^H_1(X)=\Tr(E_+(H)X)\sigma$, $\mathcal I^H_2(X)=\Tr(E_-(H)X)\sigma$, $\mathcal I^H_{k\ge3}=0$. Then $\|\mathcal I^H-\mathcal I^{-H}\|_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}=2$, witnessed by eigenvector of $H$ with eigenvalue $\pm\|H\|_\infty$ giving orthogonal flagged outputs $\sigma\otimes|1\rangle\langle1|$ vs $\sigma\otimes|2\rangle\langle2|$. The map $H\mapsto\mathcal I^{H/\|H\|_\infty}$ is continuous on $S^{d_A^2-2}$, so
\[
\alpha_r(\Inst_n(A,B))=1\quad(0\le r\le d_A^2-2)
\]
for $n\ge2,d_A\ge2$. For $n=1,d_A\ge2,d_B\ge2$, using orthogonal $\sigma_+,\sigma_-$ gives same range for channels. Joining this observable sphere, supported on $|b_0\rangle\otimes|1\rangle,|b_0\rangle\otimes|2\rangle$, with replacement sphere on orthogonal complement of flagged dimension $N_{\mathrm{rep}}^{\mathrm{obs}}=2(d_B-1)^2+(n-2)d_B^2$ (and $(d_B-2)^2$ for $n=1$) via Proposition~\ref{prop:instrument-orthogonal-join} yields dimension $d_A^2+N_{\mathrm{rep}}^{\mathrm{obs}}-3$, improving $\beta_r^{(n)}$ to $1$ up to $L_{A,B}^{(n)}=\max\{n d_B^2-2,d_A^2-2,d_A^2+N_{\mathrm{rep}}^{\mathrm{obs}}-3\}$ capped at $D_n-1$. Hence strengthened lower envelope $\beta_r^{\star,(n)}$ with $1$ for $r\le L$ and $\rho_n$ for $L<r<D_n$.
\end{theorem}

\section{Certified lower envelope and latent collisions}
\label{sec:lower-envelope}

The flagged replacement sphere and the exact full-body ball govern
different index ranges. Their combined envelope is first paired with the
intrinsic deleted-product collision condition and then specialised to latent
spaces admitting Euclidean coordinates.

\begin{corollary}[General direction-space lower bound $1/d_A$]
\label{cor:general-1dA}
For $n d_B>1$ and every $r<D_{A,B}^{(n)}$, the full direction-space Jordan construction gives a continuous $S^r\to\Inst_n$ with antipodal distance at least $2/d_A$, hence $\alpha_r\ge1/d_A$. Consequently $\beta_r^{(n)}\ge\max\{\rho_n,1/d_A\}$ for $m_n\le r<D_n$ when $\rho_n<1/d_A$, i.e. $n d_B s>2 d_A$. For large $n$, $\rho_n=O(1/n)$ while $1/d_A$ is constant, materially reducing the gap to at most $1-1/d_A$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{definition}[Certified instrument lower envelope]
For $n d_B>1$ and $r\in\mathbb N_0$, set
\[
\beta_r^{(n)}(A,B)
=\begin{cases}
1,
 &0\leq r\leq n d_B^2-2,\\[3pt]
\dfrac{2}{n d_B\min\{d_A,d_B\}},
 &n d_B^2-1\leq r<D_{A,B}^{(n)},\\[7pt]
0,
 &r\geq D_{A,B}^{(n)}.
\end{cases}
\]
The middle range is empty when $d_A=1$.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Decoder codomain hierarchy]
Because $\operatorname{RepInst}_n(A,B)\subset\Inst_n(A,B)\subset\mathfrak A_{A,B}^{(n)}$ and the triangle inequality $\|h(u)-\Psi\|+\|h(-u)-\Psi\|\ge\|h(u)-h(-u)\|$ holds for every $\Psi$ in the ambient vector space $\bigoplus_k\Lin(A,B)$, a lower bound proved for all $\Psi\in\mathfrak A_{A,B}^{(n)}$ is strictly stronger than one for $\Psi\in\Inst_n(A,B)$ only. Hence
\[
\beta_r^{(n)}(A,B)\le\delta_r^{\mathrm c}(\Inst_n(A,B);\mathfrak A_{A,B}^{(n)})\le\delta_r^{\mathrm c}(\Inst_n(A,B);\Inst_n(A,B))\equiv\delta_{r,\mathrm{inst}}^\diamond(A,B)\le U_r^{(n)}(A,B).
\]
Allowing unphysical affine decoders cannot circumvent $\beta_r^{(n)}$. The constant-code bound uses $\mathcal I_*\in\Inst_n(A,B)$, so $\inf_{\Psi\in\mathfrak A}R_{\mathrm{inst}}(\Psi)=\inf_{\Psi\in\Inst_n}R_{\mathrm{inst}}(\Psi)=R_{\mathrm{inst}}(\mathcal I_*)$.
\end{remark}

When $d_A=1$, every instrument is input-independent, so
$\Inst_n(A,B)=\operatorname{RepInst}_n(A,B)$. The exact replacement profile
in Theorem~\ref{thm:instrument-jordan-sphere} therefore describes the full
instrument body in this regime.

The two breakpoints have a direct resource interpretation. A flagged output
state has $n d_B^2-1$ affine parameters, while an input-dependent instrument
has $d_A^2(n d_B^2-1)$. The first regime is already obstructed by preparing
and recording one classical--quantum output state. The second concerns the
additional dependence of all outcome branches on the input operator.
Increasing the number of outcomes therefore has two opposite effects: it
linearly enlarges the global representation space, but it divides the local
positivity margin of the uniform instrument by $n$.

For a Hausdorff latent space $Y$, recall the deleted product
\[
F_2(Y)=\{(y_0,y_1)\in Y^2:y_0\neq y_1\},
\]
with the involution that exchanges the two entries. Its
$\mathbb Z_2$-coindex is the supremum of the sphere dimensions admitting a
continuous equivariant map into $F_2(Y)$, with value $-1$ when $F_2(Y)$ is
empty and possible value $+\infty$. This is the intrinsic latent-space invariant used in the main article. We write $\operatorname{coind}:=\operatorname{coind}_{\mathbb Z_2}$ when the group is clear.

\begin{remark}[Deleted product topology and coindex]
For a metric space $Y$, $F_2(Y)=\{(y_0,y_1)\in Y^2:y_0\neq y_1\}$ carries the subspace topology from $Y^2$ and the free $\mathbb Z_2$-action $(y_0,y_1)\mapsto(y_1,y_0)$. $\coind F_2(Y)$ is the supremum of $k$ such that there exists an equivariant map $S^k\to F_2(Y)$; if $\coind=+\infty$ no $q>\coind$ exists. If $\jmath:Y\to\mathbb R^r$ is continuous injective, $(y_0,y_1)\mapsto(\jmath(y_0)-\jmath(y_1))/\|\jmath(y_0)-\jmath(y_1)\|_2$ is continuous equivariant $F_2(Y)\to S^{r-1}$ for the product-subspace topology.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Intrinsic latent-space instrument hierarchy]
\label{cor:instrument-intrinsic}
Assume $n d_B>1$. Let $Y$ be Hausdorff and let $q\in\mathbb N_0$ satisfy
\[
q>\operatorname{coind}_{\mathbb Z_2}F_2(Y).
\]
Every continuous encoder
$\operatorname{Enc}:\Inst_n(A,B)\to Y$ and every decoder
$\operatorname{Dec}:Y\to\mathfrak A_{A,B}^{(n)}$ satisfy
\[
\sup_{\mathcal I\in\Inst_n(A,B)}
\|\operatorname{Dec}(\operatorname{Enc}(\mathcal I))-\mathcal I
\|_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}
\geq\beta_q^{(n)}(A,B).
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
There is nothing to prove when $q\geq D_{A,B}^{(n)}$. If
$0\leq q\leq n d_B^2-2$, restrict the perfectly separated replacement
sphere from Theorem~\ref{thm:instrument-jordan-sphere} to an equatorial
$S^q$. If
\[
n d_B^2-1\leq q<D_{A,B}^{(n)},
\]
put $\rho=2/[n d_B\min\{d_A,d_B\}]$, choose a $(q+1)$-dimensional subspace
$W\subseteq\mathfrak V_{A,B}^{(n)}$, and use the radial map
\[
h(u)=\mathcal I_*+\rho\,
\frac{u}{\|u\|_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}}
\]
on an auxiliary Euclidean sphere $S^q\subset W$ (the denominator is nonzero on the Euclidean unit sphere, and finite-dimensional norm equivalence makes the radial normalization continuous). Theorem
\ref{thm:instrument-ball} makes this map instrument-valued. In the first
case its antipodal separation is two; in the second it is $2\rho$.

If the two latent codes $\operatorname{Enc}(h(u))$ and
$\operatorname{Enc}(h(-u))$ were distinct for every $u$, then
\[
u\longmapsto
\bigl(\operatorname{Enc}(h(u)),\operatorname{Enc}(h(-u))\bigr)
\]
would be an equivariant map $S^q\to F_2(Y)$, contrary to the coindex
hypothesis. An antipodal pair therefore has one code. The decoder returns
one reconstruction for that pair, and the triangle inequality gives half
its separation, namely $\beta_q^{(n)}(A,B)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Continuous instrument-compression hierarchy]
\label{cor:instrument-compression}
Assume $n d_B>1$, and let $Y$ admit a continuous injection into
$\mathbb R^r$. Every continuous encoder
\[
\operatorname{Enc}:\Inst_n(A,B)\to Y
\]
and every decoder
$\operatorname{Dec}:Y\to\mathfrak A_{A,B}^{(n)}$, with no regularity
assumption on the decoder, satisfy
\[
\sup_{\mathcal I\in\Inst_n(A,B)}
\|\operatorname{Dec}(\operatorname{Enc}(\mathcal I))-\mathcal I
\|_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}
\geq\beta_r^{(n)}(A,B).
\]
For Euclidean codes, zero worst-case error is achievable if and only if
\[
r\geq D_{A,B}^{(n)}.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Let $\jmath:Y\to\mathbb R^r$ be a continuous injection. If $r=0$, then
$Y$ has at most one point, so $F_2(Y)$ is empty and has coindex $-1$. If
$r\geq1$, the normalised difference map
\[
(y_0,y_1)\longmapsto
\frac{\jmath(y_0)-\jmath(y_1)}
{\|\jmath(y_0)-\jmath(y_1)\|_2}
\]
is equivariant from $F_2(Y)$ to $S^{r-1}$. Borsuk--Ulam
\cite{Matousek2003} therefore gives
$\operatorname{coind}_{\mathbb Z_2}F_2(Y)\leq r-1$. Corollary
\ref{cor:instrument-intrinsic}, with $q=r$, proves the stated lower bound.

For the zero-error threshold, affine coordinates inject the instrument body
continuously into $\mathbb R^{D_{A,B}^{(n)}}$, and padding by zeros handles
all larger $r$. The inverse on the compact image gives exact reconstruction;
outside that image the decoder may be defined arbitrarily, e.g., as $\mathcal I_*$, which works both in the affine space and in the instrument body; no regularity is required. Conversely, the
positive lower bounds exclude exact reconstruction for every
$r<D_{A,B}^{(n)}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Constant-code instrument width]
Define
\[
\delta_{0,\mathrm{inst}}^\diamond(A,B)=\inf_{\Psi\in\Inst_n(A,B)}\sup_{\mathcal I\in\Inst_n(A,B)}\|\mathcal I-\Psi\|_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}.
\]
The lower bound in the covering theorem holds even for $\Psi$ in the full affine space $\mathfrak A_{A,B}^{(n)}$, and the minimizing center $\mathcal I_*$ is itself an instrument, so the exact constant-code width equals the covering radius.
\end{definition}

\section{Constructive upper envelope}
\label{sec:upper-envelope}

Define the Euclidean instrument width
\[
\delta_{r,\mathrm{inst}}^\diamond(A,B)
=
\inf_{\substack{f:\Inst_n(A,B)\to\mathbb R^r\ \mathrm{continuous}\\ g:\mathbb R^r\to\Inst_n(A,B)}}
\sup_{\mathcal I\in\Inst_n(A,B)}
\|g(f(\mathcal I))-\mathcal I\|_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Convex-projection instrument bound]
\label{prop:instrument-convex-upper}
For $0\le r\le D_{A,B}^{(n)}$,
\[
\delta_{r,\mathrm{inst}}^\diamond(A,B)
\le
\frac{2(D_{A,B}^{(n)}-r)}{D_{A,B}^{(n)}-r+1}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For $r=D_{A,B}^{(n)}$, affine coordinates give exact reconstruction.
Otherwise, project affine coordinates of the compact convex instrument body
onto $\mathbb R^r$ and put $k=D_{A,B}^{(n)}-r$. Each nonempty fibre $F$ is
compact and convex, has diameter at most two, and has affine dimension at
most $k$. Set $R=2k/(k+1)$ and consider $\{F\}\cup\{\overline B(x,R):x\in F\}$.
Any subfamily of at most $k+1$ members intersects: if it contains $m$ balls
centred at $x_1,\dots,x_m$, their barycentre lies in $F$ and is at distance
at most $2(m-1)/m\le R$ from each centre; if $F$ is also a member, then
$m\le k$. Helly's theorem~\cite{Barvinok2002} and compactness of $F$
therefore give a point of $F$ within distance $R$ of the entire fibre. Use
these points as decoder values.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Nonconstructive Helly decoder]
The decoder $g$ in Proposition~\ref{prop:instrument-convex-upper} is selected via Helly's theorem and compactness for each fibre $F_z$. It is guaranteed to take values in $F_z\subset\Inst_n(A,B)$ and to achieve $\sup_{x\in F_z}\|g(z)-x\|\le2k/(k+1)$, but continuity of $z\mapsto g(z)$ is not claimed and not required by the definition of $\delta_{r,\mathrm{inst}}^\diamond$, which allows arbitrary decoders outside the encoder image. The term ``constructive'' refers to explicit dimension thresholds $r^{(n)}_{\rm out},r^{(n)}_{\rm in}$ and pinching norms, not to a continuous selection. A continuous decoder would need a selection theorem.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Exact pinching norm]
\label{lem:instrument-pinching-norm}
For an orthogonal decomposition $H=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{\ell}H_j$ into nonzero
blocks and the channel $\mathcal P_H(X)=\sum_jP_jXP_j$,
\[
\|\operatorname{id}_H-\mathcal P_H\|_\diamond=2\left(1-\frac1\ell\right).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Set $\omega=e^{2\pi i/\ell}$ and $U_m=\sum_j\omega^{m(j-1)}P_j$. The roots-of-unity identity gives $\mathcal P_H=\frac1\ell\sum_{m=0}^{\ell-1}\mathcal U_{U_m}$.
The triangle inequality gives the upper bound. For equality, choose unit
vectors $|e_j\rangle\in H_j$ and apply the difference map to
$|e\rangle=\ell^{-1/2}\sum_j|e_j\rangle$. On their span the output is
$(J_\ell-I_\ell)/\ell$, whose trace norm is $2(1-1/\ell)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Input- and output-pinching for instruments]
\label{thm:instrument-pinching-upper}
The following bounds hold.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $B=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{\ell}B_j$, $b_j=\dim B_j>0$, and
\[
r_{\mathrm{out}}^{(n)}=d_A^2\left(n\sum_jb_j^2-1\right),
\]
then, for $r\ge r_{\mathrm{out}}^{(n)}$,
\[
\delta_{r,\mathrm{inst}}^\diamond(A,B)\le2\left(1-\frac1\ell\right).
\]
\item If $A=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}A_i$, $a_i=\dim A_i>0$, and
\[
r_{\mathrm{in}}^{(n)}=\left(\sum_i a_i^2\right)(n d_B^2-1),
\]
then, for $r\ge r_{\mathrm{in}}^{(n)}$,
\[
\delta_{r,\mathrm{inst}}^\diamond(A,B)\le2\left(1-\frac1k\right).
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For output pinching, the flagged Choi operators have one Hermitian block on
$A_0\otimes B_j$ for every outcome and output block. Their ambient real
dimension is $n d_A^2\sum_jb_j^2$. The summed marginal map onto
$\Herm(A_0)$ is surjective: for prescribed $H\in\Herm(A_0)$, place
$H\otimes I_{B_1}/b_1$ in one outcome block and set the others to zero,
since $\Tr_{B_1}(H\otimes I_{B_1}/b_1)=H$. Thus the marginal rank is $d_A^2$.
The uniform depolarising instrument is positive definite in the relative
affine space, so the output-pinched body has affine dimension
$r_{\mathrm{out}}^{(n)}$.

Encode the output-pinched instrument by affine coordinates and invert on the
image. The corresponding flagged channel is obtained by postcomposition
with $\mathcal P_B\otimes\operatorname{id}_{C_n}$. Diamond-norm
stabilisation, submultiplicativity, and Lemma~\ref{lem:instrument-pinching-norm} give the first error bound.

For input pinching, use the basis-conjugate decomposition
$A_0=\bigoplus_i\overline A_i$. The block-diagonal flagged Choi space has
dimension $n d_B^2\sum_i a_i^2$. The blockwise marginal map is surjective:
for prescribed $H_i\in\Herm(\overline A_i)$, place $H_i\otimes I_B/d_B$ in one
outcome block. Thus the constraints have total rank $\sum_i a_i^2$. The same
positive-definite witness gives affine dimension $r_{\mathrm{in}}^{(n)}$.
Encode $\mathcal I\circ\mathcal P_A$ by affine coordinates and invert on the
image. Submultiplicativity and Lemma~\ref{lem:instrument-pinching-norm} give
the second error bound. Padding by zero coordinates handles larger $r$ in
both cases.
\end{proof}

For $1\le\ell\le d$, write $d=m\ell+t$, $0\le t<\ell$, and set
\[
Q_\ell(d)=(\ell-t)m^2+t(m+1)^2.
\]
If two block sizes satisfy $a\ge b+2$, replacing them by $a-1$ and $b+1$
changes their squared sum by $-2(a-b-1)<0$. Iteration shows that balanced
blocks minimise the affine dimension at fixed $\ell$.

\begin{definition}[Constructive instrument upper envelope]
\label{def:instrument-upper-envelope}
For $0\le r\le D_{A,B}^{(n)}$, let $U_r^{(n)}(A,B)$ be the minimum of
\[
2\left(1-\frac1{n d_B\min\{d_A,d_B\}}\right),\qquad
\frac{2(D_{A,B}^{(n)}-r)}{D_{A,B}^{(n)}-r+1},
\]
all output-pinching values $2(1-1/\ell)$ satisfying $r\ge d_A^2(nQ_\ell(d_B)-1)$,
and all input-pinching values $2(1-1/k)$ satisfying $r\ge Q_k(d_A)(n d_B^2-1)$.
An empty pinching family is ignored.
\end{definition}

\begin{corollary}[Instrument width bracket]
\label{cor:instrument-width-bracket}
For $n d_B>1$ and $0\le r<D_{A,B}^{(n)}$,
\[
\beta_r^{(n)}(A,B)\le\delta_{r,\mathrm{inst}}^\diamond(A,B)\le U_r^{(n)}(A,B).
\]
For $r\ge D_{A,B}^{(n)}$, the width is zero.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The lower bound is Corollary~\ref{cor:instrument-compression}, specialised
to Euclidean latent space. The upper terms are Theorem~\ref{thm:instrument-covering},
Proposition~\ref{prop:instrument-convex-upper}, and Theorem~\ref{thm:instrument-pinching-upper}.
Exact reconstruction at the affine threshold was proved in Corollary~\ref{cor:instrument-compression}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Monotonicity]
\label{prop:monotonicity}
$\delta_{r,\mathrm{inst}}^\diamond(A,B)$ is non-increasing in $r$, $\beta_r^{(n)}$ is non-increasing, and $U_r^{(n)}$ defined as minimum over all admissible upper bounds at dimension $r$ is non-increasing. $\alpha_r$ need not be monotone in general, but the certified lower bounds $\beta_r^{(n)}$ and $\beta_r^{\star,(n)}$ are inherited by smaller $r$ via equatorial restriction $S^{r'}\hookrightarrow S^r$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{theorem}[Zero-error threshold]
\label{thm:zero-error-threshold}
$\delta_{r,\mathrm{inst}}^\diamond(A,B)=0$ iff $r\ge D_{A,B}^{(n)}$. The forward direction uses $\beta_r^{(n)}>0$ for $r<D_n$ from Borsuk--Ulam; the converse uses affine coordinates $c:\mathfrak A^{(n)}\to\mathbb R^{D_n}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Input-independent case $d_A=1$ as isometry]
\label{cor:dA1-isometry}
When $d_A=1$, $\Inst_n(A,B)\cong\Sstate_{\mathrm{flag}}(B\otimes C_n)$ isometrically via $\mathcal I_k(x)=x\tau_k$, $x\in\mathbb C$, $\tau\in\Sstate_{\mathrm{flag}}$, with $\|\vec{\mathcal R}_\tau-\vec{\mathcal R}_\sigma\|_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}=\|\tau-\sigma\|_1$. Hence all state-space results transfer and $\alpha_r$ equals replacement profile.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[In-radius vs antipodal profile]
$\rho_n=2/(n d_B s)$ certifies a centrally symmetric norm ball in the full direction space $\mathfrak V^{(n)}$, while $S^{n d_B^2-2}$ via $X\mapsto X_+/\Tr X_+$ certifies a non-linear antipodally $2$-separated sphere in the flagged replacement face. No contradiction from $\rho_n<1=\alpha_r$ for $r\le n d_B^2-2$; they are different geometric mechanisms.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Precise certified bracket width]
\label{prop:certified-bracket-width}
If $n d_B s>2$ and $m_n\le r<D_n$, present methods give
\[
\rho_n\le\delta_{r,\mathrm{inst}}^\diamond\le U_r^{(n)},\quad U_r^{(n)}\ge1,
\]
so certified bracket length at least $1-\rho_n$. This is the width of the certified bracket, not a proven gap for the unknown true value, unless $U_r^{(n)}$ is the optimal upper bound.
\end{proposition}

\begin{corollary}[Exact top-subcritical replacement-instrument width]
\label{cor:replacement-instrument-top-width}
Assume $n d_B>1$. Then
\[
\delta_{n d_B^2-2}^{\mathrm c}(\operatorname{RepInst}_n(A,B);\operatorname{RepInst}_n(A,B))=1.
\]
When $d_A=1$, the same equality holds for the full instrument body.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Compose any continuous encoder into $\mathbb R^{n d_B^2-2}$ with the
perfectly separated sphere from Theorem~\ref{thm:instrument-jordan-sphere}.
Borsuk--Ulam and the triangle inequality give the lower bound one. The
replacement-instrument body is compact, convex, has affine dimension
$n d_B^2-1$, and has diameter two. The Helly--barycentre argument from
Proposition~\ref{prop:instrument-convex-upper} gives the matching upper bound
at its top subcritical index. For $d_A=1$, every instrument is a replacement
instrument.
\end{proof}

\section{Exact endpoints, gap structure, and scaling}
\label{sec:endpoints-gap-scaling}

\begin{theorem}[Exact collapse when $n d_B s=2$]
\label{thm:collapse-ndBs2}
If $n d_B s=2$ with $s=\min\{d_A,d_B\}$, then $\rho_n=1$, $R_n=1$ and
\[
\delta_{r,\mathrm{inst}}^\diamond(A,B)=
\begin{cases}
1,&0\le r<D_{A,B}^{(n)},\\
0,&r\ge D_{A,B}^{(n)}.
\end{cases}
\]
This occurs for (i) arbitrary-input two-outcome POVMs $d_B=1,n=2$ any $d_A$ where $D_{A,1}^{(2)}=d_A^2$, and (ii) trivial-input qubit $d_A=1,n=1,d_B=2$ where $D_{1,2}^{(1)}=3$. Hence continuous compression of all two-outcome POVMs is exactly solved: error $1$ until full affine dimension, then $0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Asymptotic positivity-dimension invariant]
\label{cor:asymptotic-invariant}
$\Pi_n:=D_{A,B}^{(n)}\rho_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}(\mathcal I_*)=d_A^2(n d_B^2-1)2/(n d_B s)$. Then
\[
\Pi_\infty:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\Pi_n=2 d_A^2 d_B/\min\{d_A,d_B\}=2 d_A\max\{d_A,d_B\},
\]
strictly invariant under outcome branching: $O(n)$ affine growth cancels $O(1/n)$ robustness shrinkage.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[Error 1 barrier]
For $r<D_n$, every admissible constructive term satisfies $\ge1$: constant $R_n=2(1-1/(n d_B s))\ge1$ when $n d_B s\ge2$, convex $2k/(k+1)\ge1$ for $k=D_n-r\ge1$ (equality at $k=1$), pinching $2(1-1/\ell)\ge1$ for $\ell\ge2$ or $k\ge2$; $\ell=1$ gives $Q_1(d_B)=d_B^2$, $d_A^2(nQ_1(d_B)-1)=D_n$ and $k=1$ gives $Q_1(d_A)(n d_B^2-1)=D_n$, so unavailable below $D_n$. Hence no linear projection, Helly barycentre, or block-pinching scheme can achieve $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$ in the bulk $m_n\le r<D_n$. Breaking the barrier requires nonlinear manifold encodings, covering-number bounds $\log N(\varepsilon)\le r\log(1/\varepsilon)$ with $\varepsilon<1$, or orthogonal joins of full-body spheres.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Gap structure and endpoint exactness]
\label{prop:gap-structure}
Let $m_n=n d_B^2-1$, $D_n=D_{A,B}^{(n)}$, $\rho_n=2/(n d_B s)$, $R_n=2-\rho_n$.
Then $D_n+1\ge n d_B s$ because $D_n+1=d_A^2(n d_B^2-1)+1\ge n d_B^2\ge n d_B s$ for $d_B\ge1$, so at $r=0$ convex bound $2-2/(D_n+1)\ge2-2/(n d_B s)=R_n$ and $U_0=R_n$. Hence
\[
U_0-\beta_0=R_n-1=1-\rho_n
\]
is the exact certified bracket width at $r=0$. For $0<r\le m_n-1$, $\beta_r=1$ and every admissible $U_r\ge1$, so certified bracket width lower bound is $0$ but exact width for full body is only known to satisfy $1\le\delta_{r,\mathrm{inst}}^\diamond\le U_r$ unless $d_A=1$ or $n d_B s=2$. For $m_n\le r<D_n$, $\beta_r=\rho_n$ and $U_r\ge1$, so bracket width at least $1-\rho_n$, widening to $1$ as $n\to\infty$. Exact width is $0$ for $r\ge D_n$ by affine coordinates and $\alpha_r=0$ by Borsuk--Ulam. For replacement subfamily, $\delta_{m_n-1}^{\mathrm c}(\operatorname{RepInst}_n;\operatorname{RepInst}_n)=1$ exactly, and for $d_A=1$ this equals full body. Distinguish: (i) exact endpoints of full body at $r=0$ and $r\ge D_n$, (ii) exact top-subcritical of replacement subfamily at $m_n-1$, (iii) full collapse to step function when $n d_B s=2$.
\end{proposition}

\section{Specialisations and explicit trade-off}
\label{sec:specialisations}

The following explicit consequences summarise the main results.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt]
\item \textbf{Channel case $n=1$.} The instrument body reduces to the channel body, and Theorem~\ref{thm:instrument-exact} gives
\[
\rho_\diamond(\Omega_{A,B})
=\frac{2}{d_B\min\{d_A,d_B\}}.
\]
The covering radius is $2(1-1/(d_B\min\{d_A,d_B\}))$, and the lower/upper envelopes reduce accordingly.

\item \textbf{POVM case $d_B=1$, $n\ge2$.} The centred in-radius is $\rho_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}(\mathcal I_*)=2/n$. The flagged replacement sphere has dimension $n-2$ (when $n\ge2$). The affine dimension is $d_A^2(n-1)$, and the zero-error threshold is that dimension.

\item \textbf{Input-independent case $d_A=1$.} Every instrument is a replacement instrument, so the exact replacement profile $\alpha_r(\operatorname{RepInst}_n(A,B))$ from Theorem~\ref{thm:instrument-jordan-sphere} describes the full body. In particular,
\[
\alpha_r(\Inst_n(A,B))=
\begin{cases}
1,&0\le r\le n d_B^2-2,\\
0,&r\ge n d_B^2-1.
\end{cases}
\]
The centred in-radius is still $2/(n d_B)$ but is irrelevant for the antipodal profile because the body is lower-dimensional.

\item \textbf{$1/n$ suppression vs.\ $n$-fold growth.} The centred in-radius scales as $1/n$ (for fixed $d_B$) around the uniform centre $\mathcal I_*$ for the relative instrument-diamond ball with symmetric trace budget $1/(n d_A d_B)$; it does not claim that every centre or every local robustness measure scales as $1/n$. The affine dimension $D_{A,B}^{(n)}=d_A^2(nd_B^2-1)$ grows linearly in $n$. Thus more outcomes increase the number of parameters needed for exact reconstruction but reduce the local robustness of the uniform instrument.
\end{itemize}

\section{Finite-program evaluation: background}
\label{app:no-programming}

For completeness, we state the no-programming obstruction for unitary channels. The proofs are standard and are included to make the paper self-contained.

\begin{definition}[Exact finite-program evaluator]
Let $E$ and $B$ be finite-dimensional. An exact finite-program evaluator
consists of a finite-dimensional program system $M$, a completely positive
trace-preserving (CPTP) processor
\[
\Gamma:\Lin(M\otimes E)\to\Lin(B),
\]
and a program state $\pi_\Phi\in\Sstate(M)$ for every channel
$\Phi\in\Chan(E,B)$, such that
\[
\Gamma(\pi_\Phi\otimes\rho)=\Phi(\rho)
\]
for every input state $\rho$ on $E$. Equivalently, by linearity, the same
identity holds for every input operator on $E$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[No finite exact evaluator for unitary channels]
\label{thm:no-programming-app}
Let $E$ have dimension greater than one. No finite-dimensional program
system and fixed CPTP processor
$\Gamma:\Lin(M\otimes E)\to\Lin(E)$ can exactly evaluate every unitary
channel on $E$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For pure program states, the no-programming theorem
\cite{NielsenChuang1997} shows that distinct unitary channels require
orthogonal programs. It remains to pass from pure to mixed programs.

Suppose
\[
\pi_U=\sum_jq_j|p_j\rangle\langle p_j|,
\qquad q_j>0,
\]
is a spectral decomposition of a program that evaluates the unitary channel
$\mathcal U_U$. For every pure input $|\psi\rangle$, linearity gives
\[
U|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|U^\dagger
=\sum_jq_j\,
\Gamma\bigl(|p_j\rangle\langle p_j|\otimes
|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|\bigr).
\]
Every summand on the right is a state, whereas the state on the left is
pure and hence extreme. Each positive-weight summand must therefore equal
the same pure output. This holds for every pure input $|\psi\rangle$. Density operators are convex combinations of pure states, so linearity gives equality on all density operators; density operators span $\Herm(E)$ and complex linearity gives equality on all operators. Hence each $|p_j\rangle$ induces $\mathcal U_U$ exactly as a channel.

Apply the same argument to spectral decompositions of programs for two
distinct unitary channels $\mathcal U_U$ and $\mathcal U_V$. The pure-state
no-programming theorem makes every positive-weight eigenvector of the first
program orthogonal to every positive-weight eigenvector of the second.
Those eigenvectors span their respective supports, and consequently
\[
\operatorname{supp}\pi_U\perp\operatorname{supp}\pi_V.
\]
A finite-dimensional program space cannot contain infinitely many mutually
orthogonal nonzero supports, whereas a nontrivial system has infinitely many
distinct unitary channels. This proves the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Unequal output systems]
\label{cor:unequal-output-app}
Let $\dim E>1$ and $\dim B\geq\dim E$. There is no finite-dimensional exact
evaluator for all channels $E\to B$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Choose an isometry $V:E\to B$ and let
$\iota(\rho)=V\rho V^\dagger$. Fix a state $\omega$ on $E$ and define the
recovery channel
\[
\mathcal R(\sigma)
=V^\dagger\sigma V
 +\Tr[(I_B-VV^\dagger)\sigma]\,\omega.
\]
This map is CPTP and satisfies $\mathcal R\circ\iota=\id_E$. An evaluator
for all channels $E\to B$ would in particular evaluate every channel
$\iota\circ\mathcal U_U$ for the one fixed embedding $\iota$.
Postcomposing its processor with the one fixed recovery channel
$\mathcal R$ would give an exact finite evaluator for all unitary channels
on $E$, contradicting Theorem~\ref{thm:no-programming-app}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Vanishing error requires unbounded program dimension]
\label{cor:program-dimension-diverges-app}
Fix a nontrivial system $E$. For each $j$, let $M_j$ be a program system of
dimension $m_j$ and let
\[
\Gamma_j:\Lin(M_j\otimes E)\to\Lin(E)
\]
be a CPTP processor. Write
$\Gamma_{j,\pi}(X)=\Gamma_j(\pi\otimes X)$ and suppose
\[
\sup_{U\in\mathrm U(E)}
\min_{\pi\in\Sstate(M_j)}
\|\Gamma_{j,\pi}-\mathcal U_U\|_\diamond
\leq\varepsilon_j,
\qquad
\varepsilon_j\longrightarrow0.
\]
Then $m_j\to\infty$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Suppose otherwise. Passing to a subsequence, assume that
$m_j\leq m_{\max}$ for every $j$ under consideration. Let
$M\simeq\mathbb C^{m_{\max}}$ and choose an isometry
$W_j:M_j\to M$. Put $P_j=W_jW_j^\dagger$, fix
$\tau_j\in\Sstate(M_j)$, and define
\[
\Lambda_j:\Lin(M)\to\Lin(M_j),
\qquad
\Lambda_j(\eta)
=W_j^\dagger P_j\eta P_jW_j
 +\Tr[(I_M-P_j)\eta]\,\tau_j.
\]
The compression term and the measure--prepare term are completely positive,
and their traces sum to $\Tr\eta$. Thus $\Lambda_j$ is CPTP. Moreover,
\[
\Lambda_j(W_j\pi W_j^\dagger)=\pi
\qquad(\pi\in\Sstate(M_j)).
\]
The extended processor
\[
\widetilde\Gamma_j
=\Gamma_j\circ(\Lambda_j\otimes\id_E)
:\Lin(M\otimes E)\to\Lin(E)
\]
is therefore CPTP and preserves every originally programmed channel.

The set of processors of this fixed type is compact in finite dimensions.
After passing to a further subsequence, chosen independently of the target
unitary (possible because the processor sequence does not depend on $U$), suppose
$\widetilde\Gamma_j\to\Gamma$ in diamond norm. For each $U$, the program-state minimiser $\pi'_j(U)\in\Sstate(M_j)$ exists because $\Sstate(M_j)$ is compact and $\pi\mapsto\|\Gamma_{j,\pi}-\mathcal U_U\|_\diamond$ is continuous; the subsequent subsubsequence $\pi_j(U)\to\pi_U$ is $U$-dependent. Fix a unitary $U$ and choose
$\pi'_j(U)\in\Sstate(M_j)$ with approximation error at most
$\varepsilon_j$. Regard
$\pi_j(U)=W_j\pi'_j(U)W_j^\dagger$ as the corresponding embedded states on
$M$. Compactness of
$\Sstate(M)$ supplies a $U$-dependent subsubsequence converging to a state
$\pi_U$. Every such
subsubsequence of processors still converges to the same $\Gamma$.
Continuity of the induced channel in both the processor and program state
then gives
\[
\Gamma(\pi_U\otimes\cdot)=\mathcal U_U.
\]
Since $U$ was arbitrary, the one limiting processor $\Gamma$ exactly
evaluates every unitary channel, contradicting
Theorem~\ref{thm:no-programming-app}. Hence no bounded subsequence of $(m_j)$ exists. Since $(m_j)$ is integer-valued, absence of a bounded subsequence is equivalent to $m_j\to\infty$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Scope of the no-programming obstruction]
The result forbids a scheme that is simultaneously exact, deterministic,
universal over the unitary promise class, and finite-dimensional. It does
not forbid approximate, probabilistic, restricted-family, infinite-dimensional,
nor unbounded or external classical-description schemes. A finite classical
program register is still a finite-dimensional program and is not exempt
from the obstruction. Storing a Choi operator as a mathematical state is
also distinct from evaluating the represented channel with one fixed CPTP
processor. Optimal asymptotic program-cost scaling for approximate universal
unitary programming is established in~\cite{YangRennerChiribella2020}; the
compactness corollary above records the qualitative divergence consequence
needed here.
\end{remark}

\section{Structured orthogonal joins}
\label{app:instrument-joins}

The direct flagged Jordan sphere is the strongest replacement-instrument
section needed for the lower envelope. The following construction records
structured spheres supported on prescribed orthogonal output and flag blocks.

\begin{proposition}[Orthogonal join of flagged state spheres]
\label{prop:instrument-orthogonal-join}
Let $W_1,\ldots,W_\ell$ be pairwise orthogonal, flag-block-diagonal
subspaces of $B\otimes C_n$. Suppose that continuous maps
\[
h_i:S^{r_i}\to\Sstate_{\mathrm{flag}}(B\otimes C_n)
\]
take values in states supported on $W_i$ and satisfy
\[
\|h_i(u)-h_i(-u)\|_1=2
\qquad(u\in S^{r_i}).
\]
Then there is a continuous map
\[
H:S^{\sum_i r_i+\ell-1}
\to\Sstate_{\mathrm{flag}}(B\otimes C_n)
\]
with
\[
\|H(y)-H(-y)\|_1=2
\]
for every $y$. Composing with the replacement-instrument embedding gives
the same antipodal separation in instrument diamond norm.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Regard the domain sphere as the unit sphere in
$\bigoplus_{i=1}^\ell\mathbb R^{r_i+1}$. For
$y=(y_1,\ldots,y_\ell)$, put
\[
For $y=(y_1,\ldots,y_\ell)$, set $\lambda_i=\|y_i\|_2^2$ and $u_i=y_i/\|y_i\|_2$ when $y_i\neq0$. Define|For $y=(y_1,\ldots,y_\ell)$, set $\lambda_i=\|y_i\|_2^2$ and $u_i=y_i/\|y_i\|_2$ when $y_i\neq0$. Define|For $y=(y_1,\ldots,y_\ell)$, set $\lambda_i=\|y_i\|_2^2$ and $u_i=y_i/\|y_i\|_2$ when $y_i\neq0$. Define|For $y=(y_1,\ldots,y_\ell)$, set $\lambda_i=\|y_i\|_2^2$ and $u_i=y_i/\|y_i\|_2$ when $y_i\neq0$. Define|For $y=(y_1,\ldots,y_\ell)$, set $\lambda_i=\|y_i\|_2^2$ and $u_i=y_i/\|y_i\|_2$ when $y_i\neq0$. Define
\[
H(y)=\sum_{i:For $y=(y_1,\ldots,y_\ell)$, set $\lambda_i=\|y_i\|_2^2$ and $u_i=y_i/\|y_i\|_2$ when $y_i\neq0$. Defineneq0}\lambda_i h_i(u_i),
\]
with the $i$th summand set to zero when $y_i=0$. Since
$\sum_i\lambda_i=1$, this is a flagged state. Continuity at $y_i=0$
follows because the trace norm of the $i$th summand is $\lambda_i$.
Orthogonality of the supports gives
\begin{align*}
\|H(y)-H(-y)\|_1
&=\sum_i\lambda_i
\|h_i(u_i)-h_i(-u_i)\|_1\\
&=2\sum_i\lambda_i=2.
\end{align*}
Lemma~\ref{lem:replacement-instrument} transfers the equality to replacement
instruments.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Structured output--flag partitions]
\label{cor:instrument-partition}
Let $B_1,\ldots,B_\ell$ be mutually orthogonal output subspaces of
dimensions $b_i$, with $\sum_i b_i\leq d_B$. Partition the classical flag
basis into disjoint sets of cardinalities $n_i$ with $\sum_i n_i=n$.
Assume $n_i b_i^2\geq2$ for every retained block and put
\[
R=\sum_{i=1}^\ell(n_i b_i^2-1)-1.
\]
Then replacement instruments contain a continuous image of $S^R$ with
antipodal instrument-diamond distance two. Hence
\[
\alpha_r(\Inst_n(A,B))=1,
\qquad0\leq r\leq R.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
On $B_i$ and its $n_i$ assigned flags, the real vector space of
block-diagonal Hermitian operators has dimension $n_i b_i^2$. The Jordan
construction from Theorem~\ref{thm:instrument-jordan-sphere} therefore gives
a perfectly separated sphere of dimension $n_i b_i^2-2$. These state
families have mutually orthogonal supports in $B\otimes C_n$.
Proposition~\ref{prop:instrument-orthogonal-join} gives a sphere of dimension
\[
\sum_i(n_i b_i^2-2)+\ell-1
=\sum_i(n_i b_i^2-1)-1=R.
\]
The profile equality follows from equatorial restriction and the universal
diameter bound. Taking one block with $b_1=d_B$ and $n_1=n$ recovers the
leading dimension $n d_B^2-2$; thus no partition improves
Theorem~\ref{thm:instrument-jordan-sphere}.
\end{proof}

Balanced pinching & $r\ge d_A^2(n Q_\ell(d_B)-1)$ or $r\ge Q_k(d_A)(n d_B^2-1)$ & $\le2(1-1/\ell)$ or $2(1-1/k)$ & constructive, Theorem~\ref{thm:instrument-pinching-upper}\\
\hline
