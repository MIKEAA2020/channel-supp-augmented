Dear Editors,
I am pleased to submit the manuscript cited above — a 46-page, single-author research article — for consid-
eration in Quantum Information Processing.
The manuscript develops an exact, dimension-by-dimension theory of compression for quantum channels: for
channels acting between ﬁxed ﬁnite-dimensional systems, how many continuous latent parameters suﬃce to
encode an arbitrary channel and reconstruct it with uniformly small diamond-norm error, when the encoder
is continuous and the decoder unrestricted? This is a continuous analogue of nonlinear approximation the-
ory, distinct from tomography, which counts channel uses, and from programmable processors, which ﬁx a
decoding device. The optimal error is characterized as the minimal worst-case radius of the encoder’s ﬁbers.
Lower bounds follow from antipodal pairs and latent-collision topology via Borsuk–Ulam arguments and the
deleted-product coindex — even input-independent output preparation forces error one below the output-state
dimension — while the largest diamond-norm ball inside the channel body is determined exactly: centered at
the completely depolarizing channel, it is at once the optimal center and the optimal constant decoder, with
exact covering radius, so the two envelopes meet at the exact width at zero latent dimension. Constructive
upper bounds from convex projection and balanced pinching bracket the intermediate regime and close the
gap in the collapse case and from the aﬃne dimension on, where exact reconstruction is possible. Every
ﬁnite-query adaptive network induces a continuous ﬁnal code, so the same bottlenecks hold uniformly in the
number of queries, with a one-query informationally complete measurement attaining the injective threshold
and an O(N −1/2 ) repetition rate.
The question is a basic resource-theoretic one for quantum information: the memory cost of representing, simu-
lating, and communicating noisy dynamics with guaranteed worst-case accuracy. The neighbouring literature
is largely asymptotic or architecture-speciﬁc, whereas the manuscript identiﬁes exact thresholds and certiﬁed
two-sided bounds that hold for every continuous encoder of the stated latent type, with accuracy measured
in the diamond norm, the standard single-shot distinguishability distance for channels. Such architecture-
agnostic bounds delimit what any compression scheme can achieve.
A companion paper, Exact Aﬃne Geometry, Optimal Centres, Certiﬁed Compression Bounds, and Flag-
Quotient Width Obstructions for Finite-Outcome Quantum Instruments, is being submitted separately to the
journal. It treats ﬁnite-outcome instruments and proves the ﬂag-quotient ﬂatness dichotomy invoked in the
input-independent case of the present manuscript; the two papers are self-contained, cite each other explicitly,
and can be reviewed independently.
The submission lies squarely within the journal’s scope. Quantum Information Processing publishes exper-
imental and theoretical research across the entire spectrum of quantum information science, and this is a
pure quantum-information-theory contribution written in the ﬁeld’s standard formalism. The minimal latent
dimension for faithful channel representation, and the topological obstructions to attaining it, are of direct
interest to the journal’s readership in channel simulation, quantum communication, and resource-eﬃcient
information processing.
I conﬁrm that the manuscript is original, unpublished, and not under consideration elsewhere; I am its sole
author, with no funding and no competing interests to declare, and the purely theoretical work generated no
datasets.
