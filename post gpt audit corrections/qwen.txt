# Phase 1 — Fatal mathematical corrections and rigorous upgrades

## 1. Replace the false monotonicity statement with the correct theorem

The original proposition saying that \(\alpha_r\) need not be monotone is false. With the paper’s definition,

\[
\alpha_r(K)
=
\frac12\sup_{h\in C(S^r,K)}
\min_{u\in S^r} d(h(u),h(-u)),
\]

the antipodal profile is always non-increasing in \(r\).

### Correct replacement

**Proposition — Monotonicity of the antipodal profile.**  
For every nonempty metric space \(K\),

\[
\alpha_{r+1}(K)\le \alpha_r(K)
\qquad
\text{for all }r\ge0.
\]

Moreover, if \(K\) has finite diameter,

\[
0\le \alpha_r(K)\le \frac12\operatorname{diam}(K).
\]

**Proof.**  
Let \(h:S^{r+1}\to K\) be continuous. Restrict \(h\) to an equatorial copy \(S^r\subset S^{r+1}\). The antipodal map on the equator is the usual antipodal map, hence

\[
\min_{u\in S^r} d(h(u),h(-u))
\ge
\min_{u\in S^{r+1}} d(h(u),h(-u)).
\]

Taking the supremum over all continuous \(h:S^{r+1}\to K\) gives

\[
\alpha_r(K)\ge \alpha_{r+1}(K).
\]

The diameter bound is immediate because \(d(h(u),h(-u))\le\operatorname{diam}(K)\). ∎

This also strengthens the Borsuk–Ulam cutoff logic: once \(\alpha_D(K)=0\), monotonicity gives \(\alpha_r(K)=0\) for all \(r\ge D\).

## 2. Replace the unsupported “general direction-space \(1/d_A\)” corollary with a proved theorem

The audit correctly says the original corollary was not proved. The claim can in fact be repaired and made rigorous. The following theorem supplies the missing construction.

This is a genuine upgrade: it proves a full-direction antipodal lower bound for every sphere below affine dimension.

### Correct replacement

Let \(C_n\) be the classical flag space and write

\[
\mathcal C = B\otimes C_n.
\]

Let \(\Herm_{\mathrm{flag}}(\mathcal C)\) denote the real vector space of flag-block-diagonal Hermitian operators on \(\mathcal C\). The instrument body can be identified with the compact convex set

\[
\mathcal K_{A,B}^{(n)}
=
\left\{
J\in \Herm(A_0\otimes \mathcal C)_{\mathrm{flag}}:
J\ge0,\ 
\Tr_{\mathcal C}J=\frac{I_{A_0}}{d_A}
\right\}.
\]

Its direction space is

\[
\mathcal V_{A,B}^{(n)}
=
\left\{
Z\in \Herm(A_0\otimes \mathcal C)_{\mathrm{flag}}:
\Tr_{\mathcal C}Z=0
\right\},
\]

with real dimension

\[
D_{A,B}^{(n)}=d_A^2(n d_B^2-1).
\]

---

**Theorem — Full-direction antipodal sphere with separation \(2/d_A\).**  
Assume \(n d_B>1\). For every

\[
0\le r\le D_{A,B}^{(n)}-1,
\]

there exists a continuous map

\[
h:S^r\to \Inst_n(A,B)
\]

such that

\[
\|h(u)-h(-u)\|_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}
\ge
\frac{2}{d_A}
\qquad
\forall u\in S^r.
\]

Consequently,

\[
\alpha_r(\Inst_n(A,B))
\ge
\frac1{d_A}
\qquad
\text{for all }0\le r<D_{A,B}^{(n)}.
\]

If \(d_A=1\), the bound becomes \(\alpha_r\ge1\), consistent with the replacement profile.

---

### Proof

Choose any Euclidean norm on \(\mathcal V_{A,B}^{(n)}\), and let \(S(\mathcal V)\cong S^{D-1}\) be its unit sphere, where \(D=D_{A,B}^{(n)}\). It suffices to construct a continuous map on \(S^{D-1}\); restriction to equatorial copies gives all smaller \(r\).

Fix a flagged state \(\tau_0\in\Sstate_{\mathrm{flag}}(\mathcal C)\). For example, take the uniform flagged state

\[
\tau_0=\frac{I_B}{d_B}\otimes \frac{I_{C_n}}{n}.
\]

Take \(Z\in\mathcal V_{A,B}^{(n)}\), \(Z\ne0\). Write its Jordan decomposition

\[
Z=Z_+-Z_-,
\qquad
Z_+,Z_-\ge0,
\qquad
Z_+Z_-=0.
\]

Because \(Z\) is flag-block diagonal, so are \(Z_+\) and \(Z_-\). Because \(\Tr_{\mathcal C}Z=0\),

\[
\Tr_{\mathcal C}Z_+
=
\Tr_{\mathcal C}Z_-.
\]

Let

\[
t=\Tr Z_+=\Tr Z_->0,
\]

and define the normalized positive operators

\[
P=\frac{Z_+}{t},
\qquad
Q=\frac{Z_-}{t}.
\]

Then \(P,Q\) are states on \(A_0\otimes\mathcal C\), with orthogonal supports, and they have the same \(A_0\)-marginal

\[
\mu:=\Tr_{\mathcal C}P=\Tr_{\mathcal C}Q.
\]

Now define a correction state on \(A_0\). If \(d_A=1\), set

\[
J^+(Z)=P,
\qquad
J^-(Z)=Q.
\]

If \(d_A\ge2\), set

\[
\lambda=\frac1{d_A},
\]

and define

\[
\nu
=
\frac{\frac{I_{A_0}}{d_A}-\lambda\mu}{1-\lambda}.
\]

Because \(\mu\le I_{A_0}\), we have

\[
\lambda\mu
=
\frac{\mu}{d_A}
\le
\frac{I_{A_0}}{d_A},
\]

so \(\nu\ge0\). Also,

\[
\Tr\nu
=
\frac{1-\lambda}{1-\lambda}
=
1.
\]

Now define

\[
J^+(Z)
=
\lambda P+(1-\lambda)\nu\otimes\tau_0,
\]

\[
J^-(Z)
=
\lambda Q+(1-\lambda)\nu\otimes\tau_0.
\]

Both \(J^+(Z)\) and \(J^-(Z)\) are positive, flag-block diagonal states on \(A_0\otimes\mathcal C\). Their \(A_0\)-marginal is

\[
\Tr_{\mathcal C}J^\pm(Z)
=
\lambda\mu+(1-\lambda)\nu
=
\frac{I_{A_0}}{d_A}.
\]

Therefore \(J^+(Z)\) and \(J^-(Z)\) are normalized flagged Choi operators of instruments. Let

\[
h(Z)=\mathcal I^+(Z)
\]

be the instrument corresponding to \(J^+(Z)\).

For \(-Z\), the positive and negative parts are exchanged:

\[
(-Z)_+=Z_-,
\qquad
(-Z)_-=Z_+.
\]

The quantity \(t\) is unchanged, and the common marginal \(\mu\) is unchanged. Hence the correction state \(\nu\) is unchanged, and

\[
h(-Z)=\mathcal I^-(Z).
\]

The Choi difference is

\[
J^+(Z)-J^-(Z)
=
\lambda(P-Q)
\]

when \(d_A\ge2\), and

\[
J^+(Z)-J^-(Z)
=
P-Q
\]

when \(d_A=1\). Since \(P,Q\) are states with orthogonal supports,

\[
\|P-Q\|_1=2.
\]

Thus

\[
\|J^+(Z)-J^-(Z)\|_1
=
\frac{2}{d_A}
\]

for \(d_A\ge2\), and equals \(2\) for \(d_A=1\).

Finally, for any Hermiticity-preserving tuple \(\vec\Delta\), the instrument diamond norm dominates the trace norm of its normalized flagged Choi operator, because testing on the maximally entangled input gives the Choi operator itself:

\[
\|\vec\Delta\|_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}
\ge
\|\widehat J_{\widehat{\vec\Delta}}\|_1.
\]

Therefore

\[
\|h(Z)-h(-Z)\|_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}
\ge
\frac{2}{d_A},
\]

with the \(d_A=1\) case giving \(2\).

It remains only to note continuity. The map \(Z\mapsto Z_+\) is continuous on Hermitian operators. The scalar \(t=\Tr Z_+\) is continuous and strictly positive on the compact Euclidean unit sphere of \(\mathcal V\), hence bounded away from zero there. Therefore \(Z\mapsto P,Q,\mu,\nu,J^+(Z)\) is continuous. Restricting to any equatorial sphere \(S^r\subset S^{D-1}\) gives the stated result. ∎

## 3. Add the corresponding Borsuk–Ulam upper cutoff for \(\alpha_r\)

The paper should explicitly state that the full-body antipodal profile vanishes at and above affine dimension.

### Correct statement

**Proposition — Antipodal cutoff at affine dimension.**  
For \(n d_B>1\),

\[
\alpha_r(\Inst_n(A,B))=0
\qquad
\text{for all }r\ge D_{A,B}^{(n)}.
\]

**Proof.**  
Choose affine coordinates

\[
c:\Inst_n(A,B)\hookrightarrow \mathbb R^{D_{A,B}^{(n)}}.
\]

For any continuous \(h:S^r\to\Inst_n(A,B)\) with \(r\ge D\), restrict to an equatorial copy of \(S^D\) if necessary. Then

\[
c\circ h:S^D\to\mathbb R^D.
\]

By Borsuk–Ulam, there exists \(u\in S^D\) such that

\[
c(h(u))=c(h(-u)).
\]

Since \(c\) is injective,

\[
h(u)=h(-u),
\]

so the minimum antipodal distance is zero. Hence \(\alpha_D=0\), and monotonicity gives \(\alpha_r=0\) for all \(r\ge D\). ∎

## 4. Formalize the observable sphere and the join construction

The observable-sphere idea is valid, but it needs a rigorous statement and precise edge-case handling. The following is the corrected version.

---

### Theorem — Observable antipodal sphere

Assume \(n\ge2\) and \(d_A\ge2\). Fix a state \(\sigma\in\Sstate(B)\). For \(H\in\Herm_0(A)\), \(H\ne0\), define

\[
K(H)=\frac{H}{\|H\|_\infty},
\qquad
E_\pm(H)=\frac{I_A\pm K(H)}2.
\]

Define an \(n\)-outcome instrument \(\mathcal I^H\) by

\[
\mathcal I^H_1(X)=\Tr(E_+(H)X)\sigma,
\]

\[
\mathcal I^H_2(X)=\Tr(E_-(H)X)\sigma,
\]

\[
\mathcal I^H_k(X)=0
\qquad(k\ge3).
\]

Then \(H\mapsto \mathcal I^{H/\|H\|_\infty}\) is continuous on the Euclidean unit sphere in \(\Herm_0(A)\), and

\[
\|\mathcal I^H-\mathcal I^{-H}\|_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}=2.
\]

Therefore

\[
\alpha_r(\Inst_n(A,B))=1
\qquad
0\le r\le d_A^2-2.
\]

For \(n=1\), \(d_A\ge2\), \(d_B\ge2\), the same conclusion holds for channels by choosing two orthogonal output states \(\sigma_+,\sigma_-\) and defining

\[
\Phi^H(X)
=
\Tr(E_+(H)X)\sigma_+
+
\Tr(E_-(H)X)\sigma_-.
\]

---

### Proof sketch

Since \(\|K(H)\|_\infty=1\), the operators \(E_\pm(H)\) are positive effects and

\[
E_+(H)+E_-(H)=I_A.
\]

Thus \(\mathcal I^H\) is an instrument.

Because \(K(H)\) is Hermitian with operator norm one, there exists a unit eigenvector \(|\psi\rangle\) with eigenvalue \(+1\) or \(-1\). Suppose \(K(H)|\psi\rangle=|\psi\rangle\). Then

\[
\Tr(E_+(H)|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|)=1,
\qquad
\Tr(E_-(H)|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|)=0.
\]

For \(-H\), the roles of \(E_+\) and \(E_-\) are exchanged. Hence the flagged outputs are supported on orthogonal flags \(|1\rangle\) and \(|2\rangle\), giving trace distance \(2\). Since the diameter of the instrument body is at most \(2\), the distance is exactly \(2\).

Continuity follows from continuity of the operator norm and functional calculus on the unit sphere. The channel case \(n=1\) is identical, using orthogonal output states instead of orthogonal flags. ∎

## 5. Corrected orthogonal join for observable and replacement spheres

The corrupted join proof should be replaced by the following clean version.

---

### Proposition — Orthogonal join of instrument spheres

Let

\[
O:S^a\to\Inst_n(A,B)
\]

be a continuous family with

\[
\|O(u)-O(-u)\|_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}=2,
\]

and suppose all flagged outputs of \(O(u)\) are supported in a flagged subspace \(W_O\).

Let

\[
R:S^b\to\RepInst_n(A,B)
\]

be a replacement-instrument sphere supported in a flagged subspace \(W_R\) orthogonal to \(W_O\), with

\[
\|R(v)-R(-v)\|_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}=2
\]

for all \(v\), independently of the input state.

Then there is a continuous map

\[
H:S^{a+b+1}\to\Inst_n(A,B)
\]

such that

\[
\|H(y)-H(-y)\|_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}=2
\qquad
\forall y\in S^{a+b+1}.
\]

**Proof.**  
Represent \(S^{a+b+1}\) as the unit sphere in

\[
\mathbb R^{a+1}\oplus\mathbb R^{b+1}.
\]

For \(y=(x,z)\), put

\[
\lambda=\|x\|_2^2,
\qquad
1-\lambda=\|z\|_2^2.
\]

If \(x\ne0\), set \(u=x/\|x\|_2\); if \(z\ne0\), set \(v=z/\|z\|_2\). Define

\[
H(y)
=
\lambda O(u)+(1-\lambda)R(v),
\]

omitting a term when its weight is zero. Convex combinations of instruments are instruments.

Continuity at \(\lambda=0\) or \(\lambda=1\) follows because the omitted term has diamond norm bounded by its weight. Orthogonality of \(W_O\) and \(W_R\) implies that for every input state, the flagged output difference decomposes as an orthogonal direct sum:

\[
\Delta H(y)
=
\lambda\,\Delta O(u)
\oplus
(1-\lambda)\,\Delta R(v).
\]

Hence its trace norm is

\[
\lambda\|\Delta O(u)\|_1
+
(1-\lambda)\|\Delta R(v)\|_1.
\]

The replacement term has norm \(2\) for every input state. Choose an input state witnessing the antipodal distance \(2\) for \(O(u)\). Then the total norm is

\[
\lambda\cdot2+(1-\lambda)\cdot2=2.
\]

Thus \(H\) has antipodal distance \(2\). ∎

## 6. Define the explicit enlarged \(1\)-regime dimension \(L_{A,B}^{(n)}\)

Let

\[
m_n=n d_B^2-1,
\qquad
D_n=d_A^2m_n,
\qquad
s=\min\{d_A,d_B\}.
\]

The replacement sphere gives

\[
L_{\mathrm{rep}}=n d_B^2-2=m_n-1.
\]

The observable sphere gives, when valid,

\[
L_{\mathrm{obs}}=d_A^2-2.
\]

The observable-plus-replacement join gives the following dimensions.

### Case \(n\ge2\)

The observable construction uses the flagged subspace spanned by

\[
|b_0\rangle\otimes|1\rangle,
\qquad
|b_0\rangle\otimes|2\rangle,
\]

for some unit vector \(b_0\in B\). The orthogonal complement has flagged real dimension

\[
N_{\mathrm{rep}}^{\mathrm{obs}}
=
2(d_B-1)^2+(n-2)d_B^2.
\]

If

\[
N_{\mathrm{rep}}^{\mathrm{obs}}\ge2,
\]

then the replacement sphere on that complement has dimension

\[
N_{\mathrm{rep}}^{\mathrm{obs}}-2,
\]

and the join gives

\[
L_{\mathrm{join}}
=
d_A^2+N_{\mathrm{rep}}^{\mathrm{obs}}-3.
\]

If \(N_{\mathrm{rep}}^{\mathrm{obs}}<2\), ignore \(L_{\mathrm{join}}\).

### Case \(n=1\)

Here the observable construction requires two orthogonal output states and is valid when \(d_B\ge2\). The orthogonal complement has output dimension \(d_B-2\), hence flagged real dimension

\[
N_{\mathrm{rep}}^{\mathrm{obs}}=(d_B-2)^2.
\]

If

\[
N_{\mathrm{rep}}^{\mathrm{obs}}\ge2,
\]

then

\[
L_{\mathrm{join}}
=
d_A^2+N_{\mathrm{rep}}^{\mathrm{obs}}-3.
\]

Otherwise ignore \(L_{\mathrm{join}}\).

### Define \(L_{A,B}^{(n)}\)

Let

\[
L_{A,B}^{(n)}
=
\min\left\{
D_n-1,\ 
\max\left(
L_{\mathrm{rep}},
L_{\mathrm{obs}},
L_{\mathrm{join}}
\right)
\right\},
\]

where invalid terms are omitted. In particular, if \(d_A=1\), the observable and join terms are omitted and

\[
L_{1,B}^{(n)}=n d_B^2-2=D_{1,B}^{(n)}-1.
\]

## 9. Complete the covering-radius centre argument rigorously

The averaging proof should be expanded as follows.

Let \(G=\mathrm U(B)\times S_n\). For \(g=(U,\pi)\in G\), define

\[
(g\cdot\Psi)_k
=
U\circ \Psi_{\pi^{-1}(k)}\circ U^\dagger,
\]

where the output unitary acts by conjugation on \(B\). This action preserves:

1. the affine space \(\mathfrak A_{A,B}^{(n)}\);
2. the instrument body \(\Inst_n(A,B)\);
3. the instrument diamond norm.

Therefore

\[
R_{\mathrm{inst}}(g\cdot\Psi)=R_{\mathrm{inst}}(\Psi).
\]

Also \(R_{\mathrm{inst}}\) is convex in the centre:

\[
R_{\mathrm{inst}}(t\Psi_1+(1-t)\Psi_2)
\le
tR_{\mathrm{inst}}(\Psi_1)+(1-t)R_{\mathrm{inst}}(\Psi_2).
\]

Let \(\overline\Psi\) be the Haar average of \(\Psi\) over \(G\). Jensen gives

\[
R_{\mathrm{inst}}(\overline\Psi)
\le
R_{\mathrm{inst}}(\Psi).
\]

Now compute \(\overline\Psi\). First average over output unitaries. For each component \(\Psi_j\), Schur’s lemma gives

\[
\int_{\mathrm U(B)}
U\Psi_j(X)U^\dagger\,dU
=
\frac{\Tr(\Psi_j(X))}{d_B}I_B.
\]

Then average over outcome permutations. Each component becomes

\[
\frac1n\sum_{j=1}^n
\frac{\Tr(\Psi_j(X))}{d_B}I_B
=
\frac{\Tr X}{n d_B}I_B,
\]

because \(\sum_j\Psi_j\) is trace preserving. Thus

\[
\overline\Psi=\mathcal I_*.
\]

Hence

\[
R_{\mathrm{inst}}(\mathcal I_*)
\le
R_{\mathrm{inst}}(\Psi)
\]

for every \(\Psi\in\mathfrak A_{A,B}^{(n)}\). Therefore \(\mathcal I_*\) is an optimal centre. It is not necessary to claim uniqueness.

## 11. Clarify and partially strengthen the decoder convention

The paper’s width definition uses continuous encoders and unrestricted decoders. This should be stated explicitly.

However, the zero-error threshold can be strengthened: at \(r\ge D_{A,B}^{(n)}\), a continuous decoder exists.

### Correct replacement

**Theorem — Zero-error threshold with continuous decoder at threshold.**  
For Euclidean latent spaces,

\[
\delta_{r,\mathrm{inst}}^\diamond(A,B)=0
\qquad
\Longleftrightarrow
\qquad
r\ge D_{A,B}^{(n)}.
\]

Moreover, for \(r\ge D_{A,B}^{(n)}\), exact decoding can be chosen continuous.

**Proof.**  
The converse direction is unchanged: affine coordinates embed the compact convex instrument body into \(\mathbb R^{D_n}\). Let

\[
c:\Inst_n(A,B)\to C\subset\mathbb R^{D_n}
\]

be an affine homeomorphism onto a compact convex set \(C\). The Euclidean nearest-point projection

\[
\Pi_C:\mathbb R^{D_n}\to C
\]

is continuous. Define

\[
g(y)=c^{-1}(\Pi_C(y)).
\]

Then \(g\) is continuous and satisfies

\[
g(c(x))=x
\qquad
\forall x\in\Inst_n(A,B).
\]

Padding with zero coordinates gives the result for all \(r\ge D_n\).

The forward direction follows because the strengthened lower envelope satisfies

\[
\beta_r^{\mathrm{cert},(n)}(A,B)>0
\qquad
\forall r<D_n.
\]

Thus exact reconstruction is impossible for \(r<D_n\), even with arbitrary decoders. ∎

For positive-width upper bounds such as the Helly projection bound, the decoder need not be continuous. The paper should say:

> The compression widths allow continuous encoders and unrestricted decoders. The zero-error threshold remains valid even if the decoder is required to be continuous. The Helly upper bound is an existence bound unless a continuous selection theorem is additionally invoked.

This avoids misleading language without weakening the main result.
