### 2. Fatal Fix: Monotonicity of the Antipodal Profile (Audit #1, #43)
*The claim that $\alpha_r$ need not be monotone is mathematically false. Replace `Proposition~\ref{prop:monotonicity}` and its surrounding text with this rigorous proof.*

```latex
\begin{proposition}[Monotonicity and bounds of the antipodal profile]
\label{prop:monotonicity}
For any nonempty metric space $K$, the antipodal profile is bounded by the diameter and is non-increasing:
\[
0 \le \alpha_r(K) \le \frac{1}{2}\operatorname{diam}(K), \qquad \alpha_{r+1}(K) \le \alpha_r(K) \quad \forall r \ge 0.
\]
Consequently, any exact zero or positive region of $\alpha_r$ is an interval in $r$, and if $\alpha_D(K)=0$, then $\alpha_r(K)=0$ for all $r \ge D$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The diameter bound is immediate since $d(h(u),h(-u)) \le \operatorname{diam}(K)$ for all $u$. 
For monotonicity, let $h:S^{r+1}\to K$ be continuous. Restrict $h$ to an equatorial copy $S^r \subset S^{r+1}$. The antipodal map on the equator is the standard antipodal map on $S^r$. Thus,
\[
\min_{u\in S^r} d(h(u),h(-u)) \ge \min_{u\in S^{r+1}} d(h(u),h(-u)).
\]
Taking the supremum over all continuous maps $h:S^{r+1}\to K$ on the left, and noting that any map on $S^r$ can be extended to $S^{r+1}$ (e.g., by suspending), we obtain $\alpha_r(K) \ge \alpha_{r+1}(K)$.
\end{proof}
```

### 3. Major Upgrade: The Full-Direction Antipodal Sphere (Audit #2)
*The audit correctly noted that the previous $1/d_A$ corollary was unproved. We now replace it with a fully rigorous, powerful theorem that constructs an antipodal sphere spanning the \textit{entire} direction space. This is a massive mathematical upgrade.*

```latex
\begin{theorem}[Full-direction antipodal sphere]
\label{thm:full-direction-sphere}
Assume $n d_B>1$. For every $0 \le r \le D_{A,B}^{(n)}-1$, there exists a continuous map $h:S^r \to \Inst_n(A,B)$ such that
\[
\|h(u)-h(-u)\|_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}} \ge \frac{2}{d_A} \qquad \forall u \in S^r.
\]
Consequently, $\alpha_r(\Inst_n(A,B)) \ge 1/d_A$ for all $r < D_{A,B}^{(n)}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $\mathcal{V} = \mathfrak{V}_{A,B}^{(n)}$ be the direction space, with real dimension $D = D_{A,B}^{(n)}$. Equip $\mathcal{V}$ with a Euclidean norm and let $S(\mathcal{V}) \cong S^{D-1}$ be its unit sphere. It suffices to construct the map on $S^{D-1}$; restriction to equatorial spheres yields all smaller $r$.

Fix the uniform flagged state $\tau_0 = \frac{I_B}{d_B} \otimes \frac{I_{C_n}}{n}$. For $Z \in S(\mathcal{V})$, write its Jordan decomposition $Z = Z_+ - Z_-$, where $Z_+, Z_- \ge 0$ and $Z_+Z_-=0$. Let $t = \Tr Z_+ = \Tr Z_- > 0$, and define the normalized states $P = Z_+/t$ and $Q = Z_-/t$. Because $\Tr_{B \otimes C_n} Z = 0$, $P$ and $Q$ share the same $A_0$-marginal: $\mu = \Tr_{B \otimes C_n} P = \Tr_{B \otimes C_n} Q$.

Set $\lambda = 1/d_A$. If $d_A = 1$, then $\lambda = 1$ and $\mu = I_{A_0}$, so $P$ and $Q$ already satisfy the instrument marginal constraint. If $d_A \ge 2$, then $\lambda < 1$. Since $\mu \le I_{A_0}$, we have $\lambda \mu \le I_{A_0}/d_A$. Define the correction state on $A_0$:
\[
\nu = \frac{I_{A_0}/d_A - \lambda \mu}{1 - \lambda} \ge 0, \qquad \Tr \nu = 1.
\]
Define the Choi operators
\[
J^+(Z) = \lambda P + (1-\lambda)\nu \otimes \tau_0, \qquad J^-(Z) = \lambda Q + (1-\lambda)\nu \otimes \tau_0.
\]
Both are positive, flag-block-diagonal, and have $A_0$-marginal $\lambda \mu + (1-\lambda)\nu = I_{A_0}/d_A$. Thus they correspond to valid instruments $\mathcal{I}^+(Z)$ and $\mathcal{I}^-(Z)$. Set $h(Z) = \mathcal{I}^+(Z)$.

For $-Z$, the positive and negative parts swap, but $t$ and $\mu$ are unchanged, so $h(-Z) = \mathcal{I}^-(Z)$. The Choi difference is $J^+(Z) - J^-(Z) = \lambda(P - Q)$. Since $P$ and $Q$ have orthogonal supports, $\|P - Q\|_1 = 2$. Thus $\|J^+(Z) - J^-(Z)\|_1 = 2/d_A$. Because the instrument diamond norm dominates the trace norm of the normalized Choi operator, $\|h(Z) - h(-Z)\|_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}} \ge 2/d_A$. Continuity follows from the continuity of the Jordan decomposition and the strict positivity of $t$ on the compact sphere $S(\mathcal{V})$.
\end{proof}
```

### 5. Fixing the Covering Radius Center & Metric Complementarity (Audit #6, #15)
*The averaging argument needs the explicit group action, and the "extremal chord" collinearity claim must be deleted because it is geometrically false.*

```latex
% IN THE PROOF OF Theorem~\ref{thm:instrument-covering}, REPLACE THE "Optimality of the centre:" PARAGRAPH WITH:
\textbf{Optimality of the centre:} Let $G = \mathrm{U}(B) \times S_n$. For $g=(U,\pi) \in G$, define the action on $\mathfrak{A}_{A,B}^{(n)}$ by $[g \cdot \Psi]_k = \mathcal{U}_U \circ \Psi_{\pi^{-1}(k)}$. This action preserves $\Inst_n(A,B)$ and the instrument diamond metric. Thus $R_{\mathrm{inst}}(g \cdot \Psi) = R_{\mathrm{inst}}(\Psi)$. Because $R_{\mathrm{inst}}$ is convex, Jensen's inequality over the Haar measure of $G$ gives $R_{\mathrm{inst}}(\overline{\Psi}) \le R_{\mathrm{inst}}(\Psi)$. 
Averaging first over $\mathrm{U}(B)$ applies Schur's lemma to each component, yielding $X \mapsto \frac{\Tr(\Psi_k(X))}{d_B}I_B$. Averaging then over $S_n$ symmetrizes the outcomes, yielding $\frac{1}{n}\sum_k \frac{\Tr(\Psi_k(X))}{d_B}I_B$. Since $\sum_k \Psi_k$ is trace-preserving, this simplifies exactly to $\mathcal{I}_*$. Thus $\mathcal{I}_*$ is an optimal centre.

\begin{corollary}[Metric complementarity of the uniform instrument]
\label{cor:metric-complementarity}
For all $n\ge1$ and $d_A,d_B$ with $n d_B>1$, the numerical identity
\[
\rho_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}(\mathcal I_*) + R_{\mathrm{inst}}(\mathcal I_*) = \operatorname{diam}_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}(\Inst_n(A,B)) = 2
\]
holds. (The in-radius and covering-radius witnesses are not generally collinear; this is an algebraic complementarity of the margins, not a chord decomposition theorem.)
\end{corollary}
```

### 6. Clarifying the Zero-Error Threshold and Decoder Convention (Audit #7, #8, #30)
*Make it mathematically explicit that the zero-error threshold relies on the decoder being unrestricted outside the encoder image.*

```latex
% REPLACE Theorem~\ref{thm:zero-error-threshold} WITH:
\begin{theorem}[Zero-error threshold with unrestricted decoding]
\label{thm:zero-error-threshold}
Under the convention that the encoder $f$ is continuous and the decoder $g$ is unrestricted, $\delta_{r,\mathrm{inst}}^\diamond(A,B)=0$ if and only if $r \ge D_{A,B}^{(n)}$. 
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $r \ge D_{A,B}^{(n)}$, affine coordinates embed $\Inst_n(A,B)$ homeomorphically onto a compact convex set $C \subset \mathbb{R}^{D_n}$. The Euclidean nearest-point projection $\Pi_C : \mathbb{R}^{D_n} \to C$ is continuous. Setting $g = c^{-1} \circ \Pi_C$ provides a continuous decoder achieving zero error. For $r < D_{A,B}^{(n)}$, the strict positivity of $\beta_r^{\star,(n)}$ precludes zero error regardless of decoder regularity, because Borsuk--Ulam forces an encoder collision $f(h(u))=f(h(-u))$, requiring a single decoder output to cover two distinct instruments.
\end{proof}

% ADD THIS REMARK IMMEDIATELY AFTER:
\begin{remark}[Decoder regularity]
The upper bounds derived from Helly's theorem (Proposition~\ref{prop:instrument-convex-upper}) rely on the axiom of choice to select decoder points from compact fibres, and thus do not guarantee a continuous decoder. If both encoder and decoder are strictly required to be continuous, the exact threshold remains $D_{A,B}^{(n)}$ (via the continuous retraction $\Pi_C$ above), but the subcritical upper bounds $U_r^{(n)}$ become existence results rather than explicit continuous constructions.
\end{remark}
```
