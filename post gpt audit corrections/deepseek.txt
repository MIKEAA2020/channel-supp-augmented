## 1. Monotonicity of \(\alpha_r\): correction and strengthening

The original statement “\(\alpha_r\) need not be monotone in general” is false for the definition used. The correct statement is stronger and fixes the logical architecture around the Borsuk–Ulam cutoff.

Replace the proposition by:

```latex
\begin{proposition}[Monotonicity of the antipodal profile]
\label{prop:monotonicity}
Let \(K\) be any metric space. Then
\[
\alpha_{r+1}(K)\le \alpha_r(K)
\qquad(r\ge0).
\]
Consequently, if \(\alpha_D(K)=0\), then \(\alpha_r(K)=0\) for every \(r\ge D\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let \(h:S^{r+1}\to K\) be continuous. Choose an equatorial copy
\[
S^r\simeq\{(x_0,\dots,x_{r+1})\in S^{r+1}:x_{r+1}=0\}.
\]
The antipodal map on \(S^{r+1}\) restricts to the antipodal map on this equator.
Hence
\[
\min_{u\in S^r}\|h(u)-h(-u)\|
\ge
\min_{u\in S^{r+1}}\|h(u)-h(-u)\|.
\]
Taking one half, then the supremum over \(h\), gives
\[
\alpha_r(K)\ge\alpha_{r+1}(K).
\]
The final assertion follows by induction.
\end{proof}
```

This is strictly stronger than the original: it proves monotonicity of the entire profile, not just of the certified lower bounds.

## 2. Covering-radius optimality: complete group-averaging proof

The original proof of optimality of \(\mathcal I_*\) was too compressed. Replace the relevant paragraph with the following full argument.

```latex
\begin{proof}[Optimality of the centre]
We prove that \(\mathcal I_*\) minimises
\(\Psi\mapsto R_{\mathrm{inst}}(\Psi)\) over
\(\mathfrak A_{A,B}^{(n)}\).

Define the compact group
\[
G=\mathrm U(B)\times S_n,
\]
where \(S_n\) acts by permuting outcome labels. For
\((U,\pi)\in G\) and \(\Psi=(\Psi_1,\dots,\Psi_n)\), set
\[
[(U,\pi)\cdot\Psi]_k
=
\mathcal U_U\circ \Psi_{\pi^{-1}(k)}.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
[(U,\pi)\cdot\Psi](X)
=
\sum_{k=1}^n
U\Psi_{\pi^{-1}(k)}(X)U^\dagger
\otimes |k\rangle\langle k|.
\]

The action has the following properties.

\begin{enumerate}
\item It preserves \(\Inst_n(A,B)\): if \(\mathcal I\) is an instrument,
then every component \(\mathcal U_U\circ\mathcal I_{\pi^{-1}(k)}\) is
completely positive and trace-nonincreasing, and the sum over \(k\) is
the channel \(\mathcal U_U\circ\sum_j\mathcal I_j\), hence trace-preserving.

\item It preserves the instrument diamond metric. Indeed, for
\(\vec\Delta,\vec\Delta'\in\mathfrak A_{A,B}^{(n)}\),
\[
\|(U,\pi)\cdot\vec\Delta-(U,\pi)\cdot\vec\Delta'\|_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}
=
\|\vec\Delta-\vec\Delta'\|_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}},
\]
because \(\mathcal U_U\otimes\id\) and outcome permutation are
isometries of the flagged diamond norm.

\item Therefore
\[
R_{\mathrm{inst}}((U,\pi)\cdot\Psi)
=
R_{\mathrm{inst}}(\Psi).
\]

\item The function \(\Psi\mapsto R_{\mathrm{inst}}(\Psi)\) is convex on
\(\mathfrak A_{A,B}^{(n)}\). For \(\Psi_0,\Psi_1\) and
\(\lambda\in[0,1]\), and for any instrument \(\mathcal I\),
\[
\|\mathcal I-[(1-\lambda)\Psi_0+\lambda\Psi_1]\|_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}
\le
(1-\lambda)\|\mathcal I-\Psi_0\|_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}
+
\lambda\|\mathcal I-\Psi_1\|_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}.
\]
Taking the supremum over \(\mathcal I\) proves convexity.
\end{enumerate}

Now average any \(\Psi\in\mathfrak A_{A,B}^{(n)}\) over \(G\). Write
\[
\bar\Psi
=
\int_G (g\cdot\Psi)\,d\mu(g),
\]
where \(\mu\) is Haar measure. By invariance and convexity,
\[
R_{\mathrm{inst}}(\bar\Psi)
\le
\int_G R_{\mathrm{inst}}(g\cdot\Psi)\,d\mu(g)
=
R_{\mathrm{inst}}(\Psi).
\]
Hence the averaged centre is no worse.

It remains to identify \(\bar\Psi\). For each \(k\),
\[
\bar\Psi_k(X)
=
\int_{\mathrm U(B)}
U\left(
\frac1n\sum_{j=1}^n\Psi_j(X)
\right)U^\dagger
\,dU.
\]
Since the average of a completely positive trace-preserving map over
\(\mathrm U(B)\) is the uniform depolarising map,
\[
\int_{\mathrm U(B)}U\Phi(X)U^\dagger\,dU
=
\frac{\Tr[\Phi(X)]}{d_B}I_B.
\]
But \(\sum_j\Psi_j\) is trace-preserving, so
\[
\Tr\left[\sum_j\Psi_j(X)\right]=\Tr X.
\]
Therefore
\[
\bar\Psi_k(X)
=
\frac1n\cdot\frac{\Tr X}{d_B}I_B
=
\frac{\Tr X}{n d_B}I_B,
\]
which is exactly the \(k\)-th component of \(\mathcal I_*\).
Thus \(\bar\Psi=\mathcal I_*\), and
\[
R_{\mathrm{inst}}(\mathcal I_*)\le R_{\mathrm{inst}}(\Psi)
\]
for every \(\Psi\in\mathfrak A_{A,B}^{(n)}\).

This proves that \(\mathcal I_*\) is an optimal centre even over the
full ambient affine space. Since \(\mathcal I_*\in\Inst_n(A,B)\), it
follows that the constant-code width equals the covering radius about
\(\mathcal I_*\).
\end{proof}
```

This makes the group action explicit, proves invariance and convexity, and identifies the average.

## 3. Exact diameter theorem

Add a short proposition after the instrument norm definition:

```latex
\begin{proposition}[Exact instrument diameter]
\label{prop:instrument-diameter}
Assume \(n d_B>1\). Then
\[
\operatorname{diam}_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}(\Inst_n(A,B))=2.
\]
Moreover the diameter is achieved:
\begin{itemize}
\item if \(d_B>1\), by two instruments whose output states are orthogonal
for some input state and fixed outcome;
\item if \(d_B=1\) and \(n\ge2\), by two deterministic POVMs assigning
probability one to different outcomes for some input state.
\end{itemize}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Every instrument has flagged channel diamond norm \(1\), so the triangle
inequality gives diameter at most \(2\).

For the lower bound when \(d_B>1\), choose unit vectors
\(|0\rangle,|1\rangle\in B\) and an input state
\(|\psi\rangle\in A\). Define
\[
\mathcal I_1^0(X)=\Tr(X)|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|
\]
with output \(|0\rangle\) on outcome \(1\), and similarly
\(\mathcal I_1^1\) with output \(|1\rangle\). The remaining outcomes
are zero, and the sum is completed by adding the complementary
trace-nonincreasing map elsewhere. The flagged outputs for input
\(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|\) are orthogonal pure states, so the
instrument diamond distance is \(2\).

For \(d_B=1,n\ge2\), define two POVMs with effects
\(E_1=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|, E_2=0\) and
\(E_1=0,E_2=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|\). Then the flagged outputs are
orthogonal classical flags, again giving distance \(2\).
\end{proof}
```

This makes the diameter statement precise and eliminates the alternating “at most” / “exactly” language.

## 1. Continuous encoder with unrestricted decoding, and zero-error threshold with continuous decoding

Define the convention explicitly near the width definition.

```latex
\begin{definition}[Instrument compression width]
For \(r\in\mathbb N_0\), define
\[
\delta_{r,\mathrm{inst}}^\diamond(A,B)
=
\inf_{\substack{
f:\Inst_n(A,B)\to\mathbb R^r\ \mathrm{continuous}\\[1pt]
g:\mathbb R^r\to\Inst_n(A,B)
}}
\sup_{\mathcal I\in\Inst_n(A,B)}
\|g(f(\mathcal I))-\mathcal I\|_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}.
\]
The encoder \(f\) is required to be continuous. The decoder \(g\)
is unrestricted in the definition. We shall prove that for the
zero-error threshold the decoder can be chosen continuous as well.
Thus the lower bounds are stronger than necessary, and the upper
bounds are admissible under the weaker convention.
\end{definition}
```

Then replace the zero-error threshold corollary by a stronger theorem:

```latex
\begin{theorem}[Zero-error threshold for continuous encoder and continuous decoder]
\label{thm:zero-error-threshold-strong}
Assume \(n d_B>1\). For every \(r\ge D_{A,B}^{(n)}\), there exist
a continuous encoder
\[
f:\Inst_n(A,B)\to\mathbb R^r
\]
and a continuous decoder
\[
g:\mathbb R^r\to\Inst_n(A,B)
\]
such that
\[
g(f(\mathcal I))=\mathcal I
\qquad(\mathcal I\in\Inst_n(A,B)).
\]
Conversely, if \(r<D_{A,B}^{(n)}\), then for every continuous encoder
to \(\mathbb R^r\) and every decoder, continuous or not, the worst-case
error is positive.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For the achievability part, let
\[
c:\mathfrak A_{A,B}^{(n)}\to\mathbb R^{D}
\]
be an affine isomorphism. Restrict \(c\) to the compact convex body
\[
K:=\Inst_n(A,B).
\]
Because the affine dimension of \(K\) is \(D\), the image
\[
c(K)\subset\mathbb R^{D}
\]
is compact and convex with affine dimension \(D\).

For \(r=D\), define \(f=c|_K\) and let
\[
\Pi_K:\mathbb R^{D}\to c(K)
\]
be the nearest-point projection onto the compact convex set \(c(K)\).
For a nonempty closed convex subset of Euclidean space, the metric
projection is single-valued and \(1\)-Lipschitz continuous. Define
\[
g(z)=c^{-1}(\Pi_K(z))
\]
for \(z\in\mathbb R^{D}\). Then \(g(f(x))=x\) for every
\(x\in K\).

For \(r>D\), define
\[
f_r(x)=(c(x),0)\in\mathbb R^{D}\times\mathbb R^{r-D}=\mathbb R^{r}
\]
and
\[
g_r(z)=c^{-1}\bigl(\Pi_K(z_1,\dots,z_D)\bigr).
\]
Both \(f_r\) and \(g_r\) are continuous, and exact reconstruction
holds on the image.

For the converse, suppose \(r<D\). By the certified lower envelope,
for every continuous \(f\) and every \(g\), there exists an
instrument \(\mathcal I\) such that
\[
\|g(f(\mathcal I))-\mathcal I\|_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}
\ge
\beta_r^{(n)}(A,B)>0,
\]
because \(r<D\). Hence exact recovery is impossible.
\end{proof}
```

This is stronger than the original: it shows the decoder can be continuous at the exact threshold. It also uses the fact that \(c(K)\) is convex, which is correct because \(c\) is affine and injective.
