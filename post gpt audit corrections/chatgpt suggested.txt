The manuscript contains several genuine mathematical errors, unsupported claims, notation failures, and a few results that are stronger than the proofs supplied. The core affine-dimension theorem, centred-radius theorem, replacement-sphere construction, covering-radius formula, and zero-error Euclidean threshold are largely salvageable. The paper is not presently publishable as an “exact geometry” paper, however, because some later claims contradict basic topology or are stated without enough hypotheses.

I would divide the issues into **fatal**, **substantial**, and **editorial/compilation** problems.

---

# 1. Fatal mathematical error: monotonicity of \(\alpha_r\)

The proposition

> “\(\alpha_r\) need not be monotone in general”

is false for the definition given.

You define
\[
\alpha_r(K)
=\frac12\sup_{h\in C(S^r,K)}
\min_{u\in S^r}d(h(u),h(-u)).
\]

For every \(r\ge 1\), restrict any \(h:S^r\to K\) to an equatorial copy of \(S^{r-1}\subset S^r\). The antipodal map on the equator is the ordinary antipodal map on \(S^{r-1}\), hence
\[
\min_{u\in S^{r-1}}d(h(u),h(-u))
\ge
\min_{u\in S^r}d(h(u),h(-u)).
\]
Taking suprema gives
\[
\boxed{\alpha_{r-1}(K)\ge \alpha_r(K).}
\]

Thus \(\alpha_r\) is non-increasing for every metric space \(K\), not merely for the certified lower bounds.

## Required replacement

Replace Proposition~\ref{prop:monotonicity} by:

\[
\begin{proposition}[Monotonicity of the antipodal profile]
For every metric space \(K\), the sequence \(r\mapsto\alpha_r(K)\) is non-increasing:
\[
\alpha_{r+1}(K)\le \alpha_r(K),\qquad r\ge0.
\]
Consequently, every exact zero or positive region of \(\alpha_r\) is an interval in \(r\).
\end{proposition}
\]

This also strengthens the logic around the Borsuk--Ulam cutoff: once \(\alpha_D=0\), all \(\alpha_r\) with \(r\ge D\) vanish automatically by monotonicity.

# 2. The “general direction-space lower bound \(1/d_A\)” is not proved and is probably not valid as stated

The corollary claims:

> “For \(n d_B>1\) and every \(r<D_{A,B}^{(n)}\), the full direction-space Jordan construction gives a continuous \(S^r\to\Inst_n\) with antipodal distance at least \(2/d_A\), hence \(\alpha_r\ge1/d_A\).”

No construction is actually supplied. More importantly, the obvious Jordan construction does **not** immediately produce instruments.

The direction-space Choi operator has the form
\[
X=\widehat J_{\vec\Delta}
=\sum_k X_k\otimes |k\rangle\langle k|
\]
on
\[
A_0\otimes B\otimes C_n,
\]
with
\[
\sum_k\Tr_B X_k=0.
\]
Its positive and negative parts \(X_+\) and \(X_-\) are positive operators on
\[
A_0\otimes B\otimes C_n,
\]
not on \(B\otimes C_n\). Therefore
\[
X\mapsto \frac{X_+}{\Tr X_+}
\]
produces a state involving the reference/input Choi space \(A_0\); it does not directly produce a replacement instrument.

The replacement embedding only accepts states on \(B\otimes C_n\). There is no stated map
\[
\Herm_0(\mathfrak V_{A,B}^{(n)})
\longrightarrow
\Herm_0(B\otimes C_n)
\]
that preserves the required antipodal separation and has the claimed dimension \(D_{A,B}^{(n)}-1\).

The bound may be true under a different construction, but it is not established here.

## Required action

Either:

1. delete Corollary~\ref{cor:general-1dA}; or
2. provide an explicit construction and prove:
   - instrument-valuedness,
   - continuity,
   - the claimed dimension,
   - the claimed lower bound in the instrument diamond norm.

The corollary is not used in the definition of \(\beta_r^{(n)}\), so deleting it does not damage the main bracket.

# 3. The theorem “full exact metric parameters” overclaims what is actually determined

The abstract says:

> “We determine the exact affine and metric parameters of the compact convex body…”

But the manuscript does **not** determine the full metric geometry. It determines:

- affine dimension;
- centred in-radius at one specific centre \(\mathcal I_*\);
- covering radius about \(\mathcal I_*\), and apparently globally, if the centre argument is accepted;
- a replacement-family antipodal profile;
- lower and upper bounds for the full continuous compression width.

It does **not** determine:

- the global in-radius;
- the exact \(\alpha_r(\Inst_n(A,B))\) in the intermediate range;
- the exact \(\delta_{r,\mathrm{inst}}^\diamond\) for most \(0<r<D\);
- the exact geometry of the full instrument body under the instrument diamond norm.

The manuscript itself admits that the global in-radius is open:

> “The global in-radius … could be larger…”

That directly conflicts with “exact metric parameters of the compact convex body.”

## Required replacement

Use:

> “We determine the affine dimension, the centred in-radius at the uniform depolarising instrument, the global covering radius, and certified lower and upper bounds for continuous compression.”

Do not call the full metric geometry exact.

# 4. The observable-sphere theorem contains an undefined object and an unproved improvement

The theorem states:

> “Hence strengthened lower envelope \(\beta_r^{\star,(n)}\) with \(1\) for \(r\le L\) and \(\rho_n\) for \(L<r<D_n\).”

But \(\beta_r^{\star,(n)}\) is never formally defined. The preceding definition only defines
\[
\beta_r^{(n)}.
\]

The theorem also says the orthogonal join “improves” the lower envelope, but no exact piecewise formula is given and no comparison is made between the join dimension and \(n d_B^2-2\) in all parameter regimes.

There are further edge cases:

- \(n=1\);
- \(d_B=1\);
- \(d_B=2\), where the claimed complement may have zero dimension;
- \(d_A=1\), where the input-observable construction is unavailable;
- \(N_{\mathrm{rep}}^{\mathrm{obs}}\le 1\), where a sphere of the claimed dimension does not exist.

## Required replacement

Define explicitly:
\[
\beta_r^{\star,(n)}(A,B)
=
\begin{cases}
1,&0\le r\le L_{A,B}^{(n)},\\
\rho_n,&L_{A,B}^{(n)}<r<D_n,\\
0,&r\ge D_n,
\end{cases}
\]
provided \(L_{A,B}^{(n)}<D_n\), and otherwise set the middle interval empty.

Then state and prove all dimension conditions required for the join. In particular, a sphere \(S^{q}\) exists only for \(q\ge0\), so every component used in the join must satisfy
\[
n_i b_i^2\ge2
\]
and the observable component must have the stated dimension.

# 5. The orthogonal-join proof is literally corrupted and will not compile

The proof contains the following malformed repeated text:

```latex
For $y=(y_1,\ldots,y_\ell)$, set ...
...
Define|
...
```

This is not merely a typographical issue: the document will not compile correctly.

The intended proof should be:

```latex
For \(y=(y_1,\ldots,y_\ell)\), set
\[
\lambda_i=\|y_i\|_2^2,
\qquad
u_i=\frac{y_i}{\|y_i\|_2}
\quad\text{when }y_i\ne0.

Define
\[
H(y)=\sum_{i:y_i\ne0}\lambda_i h_i(u_i).
\]
```

The continuity argument is correct in spirit, but should explicitly use
\[
\|\lambda_i h_i(u_i)\|_1=\lambda_i\to0
\]
as \(y_i\to0\).

# 6. The exact covering-radius theorem needs a more careful centre argument

The theorem claims that \(\mathcal I_*\) is an optimal centre even over the full affine space. The averaging idea is plausible, but the proof is too compressed.

You need to define the group action precisely. For \(U\in\mathrm U(B)\) and a permutation \(\pi\in S_n\), define
\[
[(U,\pi)\cdot\Psi]_k
=
\mathcal U_U\circ \Psi_{\pi^{-1}(k)}.
\]
Then prove:

1. the action preserves \(\Inst_n(A,B)\);
2. it preserves the instrument diamond metric;
3. \(R_{\mathrm{inst}}\) is invariant under the action;
4. \(R_{\mathrm{inst}}\) is convex in the centre \(\Psi\);
5. averaging over \(\mathrm U(B)\times S_n\) yields \(\mathcal I_*\).

The current phrase

> “Averaging any \(\Psi\) first over output unitaries and then over outcome permutations…”

is not enough for an exact theorem.

There is also a subtle issue: the output-unitary average of an arbitrary affine map \(\Psi_k\) is determined by its input effect
\[
E_k=\Tr_B \widehat J_{\Psi_k}.
\]
Only after permutation averaging do you obtain
\[
\frac1n\sum_k E_k=\frac{I_A}{n}.
\]
This should be shown explicitly.

# 7. “Exact constant-code width” is correct only under the paper’s asymmetric decoder convention

You define
\[
\delta_{r,\mathrm{inst}}^\diamond
=
\inf_{f\ \mathrm{continuous},\,g\ \mathrm{arbitrary}}
\sup_{\mathcal I}
d(g(f(\mathcal I)),\mathcal I).
\]

Thus the decoder is not required to be continuous. The Helly construction exploits this.

The manuscript repeatedly calls the result a “continuous compression” result. That is potentially misleading. Only the encoder is continuous. The decoder is arbitrary in the main width definition.

This distinction matters substantially:

- If both encoder and decoder must be continuous, the Helly selection argument is insufficient.
- A continuous selection from the fibres is not automatic.
- The exact zero-error threshold for continuous encoder **and continuous decoder** is not established by the present proof, although an affine embedding plus a continuous retraction onto the compact convex image may be possible if constructed carefully.

## Required clarification

Change the terminology to:

> “continuous encoding with unrestricted decoding”

throughout the width sections, or impose continuity on \(g\) and prove the needed selection/retraction result.

That is fine. But the text should explicitly say:

g(f(h(u)))=g(f(h(-u)))

This is a useful strength of the theorem, but it should be stated instead of leaving readers to infer it.

# 13. The covering-radius domination proof should distinguish the reference rank from the output dimension more cleanly

The lemma proves
\[
\sigma_{RB}\le \min\{r,d_B\}\rho_R\otimes I_B.
\]

The proof uses a decomposition
\[
Z=\sum_\alpha |K_\alpha\rangle\!\rangle\langle\!\langle K_\alpha|
\]
with
\[
\sum_\alpha K_\alpha^\dagger K_\alpha=I_R.
\]

This is fine, but the statement “\(r=\rank\rho_R\)” and then the use of \(\rank(V)\le\min\{r,d_B\}\) should be made explicit after restricting \(R\) to \(\supp\rho_R\). Otherwise \(V\) appears to act on the full reference space while its rank bound uses the restricted space.

A cleaner proof is:
\[
\rho_R^{-1/2}\sigma_{RB}\rho_R^{-1/2}=Z,\qquad \Tr_B Z=I_{\supp\rho_R},
\]
then for every unit vector \(v\in\supp\rho_R\otimes B\), Schmidt rank\((v)\le\min\{r,d_B\}\), and the quadratic-form bound follows.

# 14. The “diameter \(=2\)” statements need to distinguish \(\le2\) from \(=2\)

The table says:

- for \(d_B>1\), by channels/instruments producing orthogonal output states;

State this once as a proposition:
\[
\operatorname{diam}_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}\Inst_n(A,B)=2
\qquad(nd_B>1).

The current text alternates between “at most two” and exact diameter without making the equality theorem explicit.

# 15. The “metric complementarity” corollary is phrased geometrically more strongly than proved

The corollary says:

> “The uniform depolarising instrument divides the extremal chord joining the boundary point \(\mathcal I_*+\rho_n\vec\Delta\) and the extreme pure instrument at distance \(R_n\) in ratio \(\rho_n:(2-\rho_n)\).”

The two points are not shown to lie on the same chord through \(\mathcal I_*\). The radius witness and covering-radius witness are constructed independently. There is no reason the direction \(\vec\Delta\) that attains the in-radius and the pure instrument that attains the covering radius are collinear.

The equality
\[
\rho_n+R_n=2
\]
is algebraic. It does not imply the existence of one extremal chord realizing both quantities.

## Required deletion

Delete the “extremal chord” sentence, or prove collinearity explicitly. The safe statement is:
\[
\rho_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}(\mathcal I_*)
+
R_{\mathrm{inst}}(\mathcal I_*)=2,
\]
which is a numerical identity, not a chord decomposition theorem.

# 18. The input-pinching affine dimension needs an explicit conjugation convention

The proof says:

This is correct for the normalized Choi convention, but the paper should define

\mathcal P_A(X)=\sum_i P_iXP_i

\widehat J_{\Phi\circ\mathcal P_A}

# 19. The affine dimension proof should distinguish positivity from complete positivity more consistently

The proof says:

> “Since every Choi block of \(\mathcal I_*\) is positive definite, sufficiently small perturbations … remain block-positive…”

Use “positive definite,” not “block-positive.” “Block-positive” has a different technical meaning in quantum information: positivity on product vectors, not positive semidefiniteness.

The exact statement is:
\[
\widehat J_{(\mathcal I_*)_k}\succ0,
\]
so every sufficiently small Hermitian perturbation remains positive semidefinite.

# 20. The no-programming appendix is mostly valid, but the mixed-program argument should explicitly invoke purity of every output summand

The proof writes
\[
U|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|U^\dagger
=
\sum_j q_j\Gamma(|p_j\rangle\langle p_j|\otimes|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|).
\]

Each summand has trace one because \(\Gamma\) is CPTP. Since the convex combination is rank one, every positive-weight summand equals the same pure state. This is correct.

However, the sentence

> “This holds for every pure input \(|\psi\rangle\). Density operators are convex combinations…”

should explicitly explain that the induced channel from each \(|p_j\rangle\) agrees with \(\mathcal U_U\) on all pure-state projectors, hence on all operators by linearity.

The theorem is salvageable without major alteration.

# 21. The approximate-program-dimension proof is valid in broad outline but its quantifiers should be tightened

The corollary assumes
\[
\sup_U\min_\pi
\|\Gamma_{j,\pi}-\mathcal U_U\|_\diamond\le\varepsilon_j.
\]

The proof chooses, for each fixed \(U\), a subsequence of program states. That is sufficient to produce one limiting program state \(\pi_U\) for each \(U\), because the limiting processor \(\Gamma\) is common.

However, make clear that:

- the processor subsequence is selected before fixing \(U\);
- the \(U\)-dependent subsubsequence is used only to obtain \(\pi_U\);
- compactness of the program state space is applied separately for each \(U\).

The present proof does say this, but the quantifier structure is buried.

Also, the map
\[
\Lambda_j(\eta)
=
W_j^\dagger P_j\eta P_jW_j
+
\Tr[(I-P_j)\eta]\tau_j
\]
should use \(\Tr[(I-P_j)\eta]\tau_j\) only for Hermitian \(\eta\); to establish complete positivity, write the second term as a measure-and-prepare map:
\[
\eta\mapsto \Tr[(I-P_j)\eta]\tau_j.
\]
This is CP because \(I-P_j\ge0\).

# 37. The instrument norm operational interpretation has a factor-of-two error

The remark says:

> “\(\|\vec\Delta\|_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}\) equals the optimal bias in discriminating two instruments…”

For two quantum channels/instruments with equal priors, the optimal success probability is
\[
p_{\mathrm{succ}}
=
\frac12+\frac14\|\mathcal I-\mathcal J\|_\diamond.
\]
The conventional distinguishing advantage over random guessing is
\[
p_{\mathrm{succ}}-\frac12
=
\frac14\|\mathcal I-\mathcal J\|_\diamond.
\]

If by “bias” you mean the unnormalized trace-distance bias, some communities use
\[
\frac12\|\mathcal I-\mathcal J\|_\diamond.
\]
But the manuscript must choose a convention. The norm itself is not “the optimal bias” without a factor.

Replace with:
> “For equal priors, the optimal discrimination success probability is \(1/2+\|\mathcal I-\mathcal J\|_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}/4\).”

# 38. The statement \(\|\Phi\otimes\id_C\|_\diamond=\|\Phi\|_\diamond\) needs the domain/codomain orientation

The equality is true for the diamond norm, but the paper writes it in a context where \(\Phi\) is sometimes the difference of instrument maps and sometimes a replacement map. Define the spaces explicitly to avoid a type mismatch.

# A clean corrected theorem architecture

The paper would be much stronger if reorganized around the following theorem sequence.

## Theorem 1: affine geometry

Let
\[
\mathsf H_{A,B}^{(n)}
=
\bigoplus_{k=1}^n\Herm(A_0\otimes B).
\]
The marginal map
\[
T(X_1,\dots,X_n)=\sum_k\Tr_B X_k
\]
is surjective, hence
\[
\dim\ker T
=
n d_A^2d_B^2-d_A^2
=
d_A^2(nd_B^2-1).
\]
The uniform instrument is relative interior because all Choi blocks are positive definite.

This proves:
\[
\boxed{D_n=d_A^2(nd_B^2-1).}
\]

## Theorem 2: centred in-radius

For \(s=\min(d_A,d_B)\),
\[
\boxed{\rho_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}(\mathcal I_*)
=\frac{2}{n d_Bs}}
\]
when \(n d_B>1\), with the explicit two-case boundary witness:
- orthogonal output isometries if \(d_B>1\);
- outcome-transfer POVM witness if \(d_B=1,n\ge2\).

## Theorem 3: covering radius

Prove:
\[
\boxed{
\sup_{\mathcal I\in\Inst_n(A,B)}
\|\mathcal I-\mathcal I_*\|_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}
=
2\left(1-\frac1{n d_Bs}\right).
}
\]
Then complete the averaging proof that \(\mathcal I_*\) is an optimal centre.

## Theorem 4: replacement profile

For
\[
m=n d_B^2-1,
\]
\[
\boxed{
\alpha_r(\RepInst_n)=
\begin{cases}
1,&0\le r\le m-1,\\
0,&r\ge m.
\end{cases}}
\]

## Theorem 5: full-body lower envelope

Define
\[
\beta_r^{(n)}
=
\begin{cases}
1,&0\le r\le m-1,\\
\rho_n,&m\le r<D_n,\\
0,&r\ge D_n.
\end{cases}
\]
Then
\[
\beta_r^{(n)}
\le
\delta_{r,\mathrm{inst}}^\diamond.
\]

If retaining the observable join, define a separate rigorously proved strengthened envelope rather than mentioning it informally.

## Theorem 6: exact zero-error Euclidean threshold

Under the manuscript’s convention that the encoder is continuous and the decoder is unrestricted:
\[
\boxed{
\delta_{r,\mathrm{inst}}^\diamond=0
\iff r\ge D_n.
}
\]

Add a separate remark:

> If continuity of the decoder is required, the present proof does not establish the same theorem.

## Proposition 7: monotonicity

Correct to:
\[
\boxed{\alpha_{r+1}(K)\le\alpha_r(K).}
\]

# Most important conceptual correction

The central valid result is not “the complete exact geometry of instruments.” It is:

> The instrument body has exact affine dimension \(d_A^2(nd_B^2-1)\), the uniform instrument has exact centred instrument-diamond in-radius \(2/(nd_B\min\{d_A,d_B\})\), its global covering radius is \(2(1-1/(nd_B\min\{d_A,d_B\}))\), and these yield rigorous lower and upper bounds on continuous-encoder compression widths.

That is already a coherent and publishable theorem package. The manuscript becomes substantially stronger if it stops claiming exactness for the unresolved intermediate compression widths and removes the unsupported direction-space corollary.
