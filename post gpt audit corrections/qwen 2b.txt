### 2. Rigorous Proof of the Full-Direction $1/d_A$ Antipodal Sphere
**Location:** Section~\ref{sec:lower-envelope} (or a new dedicated subsection).
**Issue:** The audit correctly identifies that the previous manuscript claimed a general direction-space lower bound of $1/d_A$ without providing the requisite map from the direction space to the state space that preserves the marginal constraint. 
**Action:** Insert the following fully rigorous theorem and proof. This elevates the paper by proving a non-trivial topological obstruction across the *entire* direction space, not just the replacement face.

```latex
\begin{theorem}[Full-direction antipodal sphere]
\label{thm:full-direction-sphere}
Assume $n d_B > 1$. For every integer $0 \le r \le D_{A,B}^{(n)}-1$, there exists a continuous map $h: S^r \to \Inst_n(A,B)$ such that
\[
\|h(u) - h(-u)\|_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}} \ge \frac{2}{d_A}
\qquad \forall u \in S^r.
\]
Consequently, $\alpha_r(\Inst_n(A,B)) \ge 1/d_A$ for all $r < D_{A,B}^{(n)}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $\mathcal{V} = \mathfrak{V}_{A,B}^{(n)}$ be the direction space, equipped with any Euclidean norm, and let $S(\mathcal{V}) \cong S^{D-1}$ be its unit sphere, where $D = D_{A,B}^{(n)}$. It suffices to construct the map on $S^{D-1}$; restriction to equatorial copies yields all smaller $r$.

Fix a reference flagged state $\tau_0 = \frac{I_B}{d_B} \otimes \frac{I_{C_n}}{n} \in \Sstate_{\mathrm{flag}}(B \otimes C_n)$. For any nonzero $Z \in \mathcal{V}$, consider its Jordan decomposition $Z = Z_+ - Z_-$, where $Z_+, Z_- \ge 0$ and $Z_+ Z_- = 0$. Because $Z$ is flag-block-diagonal, so are $Z_\pm$. The trace-annihilating constraint $\Tr_{B C_n} Z = 0$ implies that the $A_0$-marginals of $Z_+$ and $Z_-$ are identical:
\[
\mu := \Tr_{B C_n} Z_+ = \Tr_{B C_n} Z_-.
\]
Let $t = \Tr Z_+ = \Tr Z_- > 0$, and define the normalized states $P = Z_+/t$ and $Q = Z_-/t$. Both $P$ and $Q$ are states on $A_0 \otimes B \otimes C_n$ with identical $A_0$-marginal $\mu/t$.

Set $\lambda = 1/d_A$. Because $\mu/t \le I_{A_0}$, we have $\lambda (\mu/t) \le I_{A_0}/d_A$. Thus, the operator
\[
\nu := \frac{\frac{I_{A_0}}{d_A} - \lambda \frac{\mu}{t}}{1 - \lambda}
\]
is positive semi-definite and has unit trace, making it a valid state on $A_0$. Now define the Choi operators:
\[
J^+(Z) = \lambda P + (1-\lambda) \nu \otimes \tau_0, \qquad
J^-(Z) = \lambda Q + (1-\lambda) \nu \otimes \tau_0.
\]
Both $J^\pm(Z)$ are positive, flag-block-diagonal, and their $A_0$-marginal is exactly $\lambda(\mu/t) + (1-\lambda)\nu = I_{A_0}/d_A$. Hence, they are the normalized flagged Choi operators of valid instruments $\mathcal{I}^\pm(Z) \in \Inst_n(A,B)$.

For the antipodal point $-Z$, the positive and negative parts are swapped, so $J^+(-Z) = J^-(Z)$. The difference is
\[
J^+(Z) - J^-(Z) = \lambda(P - Q).
\]
Since $P$ and $Q$ have orthogonal supports, $\|P - Q\|_1 = 2$. Therefore, the trace norm of the Choi difference is $2\lambda = 2/d_A$. Because the instrument diamond norm lower-bounds the trace norm of the normalized Choi operator (by testing on the maximally entangled state), we obtain
\[
\|\mathcal{I}^+(Z) - \mathcal{I}^-(Z)\|_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}} \ge \frac{2}{d_A}.
\]
Continuity of the map $Z \mapsto \mathcal{I}^+(Z)$ on the compact Euclidean sphere follows from the continuity of the Jordan decomposition and the strict positivity of $t$ away from the origin.
\end{proof}
```

### 3. Rigorous Covering-Radius Center Averaging
**Location:** Section~\ref{sec:covering}, Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:instrument-covering}.
**Issue:** The audit notes the group action and convexity arguments were too compressed.
**Action:** Replace the "Optimality of the centre" paragraph at the end of the proof with this expanded, rigorous version:

```latex
\textit{Optimality of the centre:} Let $G = \mathrm{U}(B) \times S_n$. For $g = (U, \pi) \in G$, define the action on $\mathfrak{A}_{A,B}^{(n)}$ by
\[
[(U, \pi) \cdot \Psi]_k = \mathcal{U}_U \circ \Psi_{\pi^{-1}(k)},
\]
where $\mathcal{U}_U(X) = U X U^\dagger$. This action preserves the affine space $\mathfrak{A}_{A,B}^{(n)}$, the instrument body $\Inst_n(A,B)$, and the instrument diamond metric. Consequently, $R_{\mathrm{inst}}(g \cdot \Psi) = R_{\mathrm{inst}}(\Psi)$. Furthermore, $R_{\mathrm{inst}}(\Psi) = \sup_{\mathcal{I}} \|\mathcal{I} - \Psi\|_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}$ is convex in $\Psi$.

Let $\overline{\Psi}$ be the average of $\Psi$ over $G$ using the Haar measure on $\mathrm{U}(B)$ and the uniform measure on $S_n$. By Jensen's inequality, $R_{\mathrm{inst}}(\overline{\Psi}) \le R_{\mathrm{inst}}(\Psi)$. We compute $\overline{\Psi}$ in two steps. First, averaging over $\mathrm{U}(B)$ applies the completely depolarising channel to the output of each component, yielding $X \mapsto \frac{\Tr(\Psi_k(X))}{d_B} I_B$. Second, averaging over $S_n$ symmetrises the outcomes, yielding
\[
\overline{\Psi}_k(X) = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{j=1}^n \frac{\Tr(\Psi_j(X))}{d_B} I_B = \frac{\Tr(X)}{n d_B} I_B,
\]
where the final equality uses the trace-preserving property $\sum_j \Psi_j = \id$. Thus $\overline{\Psi} = \mathcal{I}_*$, proving $R_{\mathrm{inst}}(\mathcal{I}_*) \le R_{\mathrm{inst}}(\Psi)$ for all $\Psi \in \mathfrak{A}_{A,B}^{(n)}$.
```

### 5. Monotonicity of the Antipodal Profile
**Location:** Section~\ref{sec:replacement}, immediately after Definition~\ref{def:antipodal-profile} (or wherever $\alpha_r$ is defined).
**Issue:** The mathematical properties of $\alpha_r$ should be explicitly stated to justify the cutoff logic.
**Action:** Insert this short, rigorous proposition.

```latex
\begin{proposition}[Monotonicity and diameter bound]
\label{prop:alpha-monotone}
For any nonempty metric space $K$ of finite diameter, the antipodal profile is non-increasing:
\[
\alpha_{r+1}(K) \le \alpha_r(K) \qquad \forall r \ge 0,
\]
and bounded by $0 \le \alpha_r(K) \le \frac{1}{2}\operatorname{diam}(K)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $h: S^{r+1} \to K$ be continuous. Restrict $h$ to an equatorial copy $S^r \subset S^{r+1}$. The antipodal map on the equator coincides with the standard antipodal map on $S^r$. Thus,
\[
\min_{u \in S^r} d(h(u), h(-u)) \ge \min_{u \in S^{r+1}} d(h(u), h(-u)).
\]
Taking the supremum over all continuous maps on $S^{r+1}$ yields $\alpha_r(K) \ge \alpha_{r+1}(K)$. The diameter bound is immediate since $d(x,y) \le \operatorname{diam}(K)$ for all $x,y \in K$.
\end{proof}
```
