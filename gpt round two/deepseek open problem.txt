The open question in Remark 3.4 is resolved affirmatively: the uniform depolarising instrument \(\mathcal I_*\) is in fact an optimal centre for the global in-radius.

### Theorem

Assume \(n d_B>1\). Then

\[
\sup_{\mathcal I_0\in\Inst_n(A,B)}
\rho_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}(\mathcal I_0)
=
\rho_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}(\mathcal I_*)
=
\frac{2}{n d_B\min\{d_A,d_B\}}.
\]

### Proof

Define the in-radius functional

\[
f(\mathcal I_0)
:=
\rho_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}(\mathcal I_0)
=
\sup\Bigl\{
\rho\ge 0:
\mathcal I_0+\mathfrak V_{A,B}^{(n)}\cap
\overline B_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}(0,\rho)
\subseteq
\Inst_n(A,B)
\Bigr\}.
\]

Here \(\overline B_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}(0,\rho)\) is the closed ball in the direction space \(\mathfrak V_{A,B}^{(n)}\).

#### Step 1: \(f\) is concave

Let \(\mathcal I_0,\mathcal I_1\in\Inst_n(A,B)\), let \(\lambda\in[0,1]\), and suppose

\[
\mathcal I_j+\rho_j B_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}(0,1)
\subseteq
\Inst_n(A,B),
\qquad j=0,1.
\]

Then by convexity of \(\Inst_n(A,B)\),

\[
\lambda(\mathcal I_0+\rho_0 B)
+
(1-\lambda)(\mathcal I_1+\rho_1 B)
=
(\lambda\mathcal I_0+(1-\lambda)\mathcal I_1)
+
(\lambda\rho_0+(1-\lambda)\rho_1)B
\subseteq
\Inst_n(A,B).
\]

Taking suprema over admissible \(\rho_0,\rho_1\) gives

\[
f\bigl(\lambda\mathcal I_0+(1-\lambda)\mathcal I_1\bigr)
\ge
\lambda f(\mathcal I_0)+(1-\lambda)f(\mathcal I_1).
\]

Thus \(f\) is concave.

#### Step 2: \(f\) is invariant under the symmetrisation group

Let \(G=\mathrm U(B)\times S_n\) act on instruments by

\[
[(U,\pi)\cdot \mathcal I]_k
=
\mathcal U_U\circ \mathcal I_{\pi^{-1}(k)},
\qquad
\mathcal U_U(X)=UXU^\dagger.
\]

This action has the following properties:

1. It maps \(\Inst_n(A,B)\) bijectively onto itself.
2. It maps the direction space \(\mathfrak V_{A,B}^{(n)}\) bijectively onto itself, because
   \[
   \Tr_B\bigl[(I_{A_0}\otimes U)
   \Jhat_{\Delta_k}
   (I_{A_0}\otimes U^\dagger)\bigr]
   =
   \Tr_B\Jhat_{\Delta_k},
   \]
   and permutations of the outcome index preserve the summed trace-annihilating condition.
3. It is an isometry for the instrument diamond norm, since it acts by a unitary/permutation channel on the flagged output system \(B\otimes C_n\).

Consequently,

\[
f(g\cdot \mathcal I)=f(\mathcal I)
\]

for all \(g\in G\) and all \(\mathcal I\in\Inst_n(A,B)\).

#### Step 3: Symmetrisation gives \(\mathcal I_*\)

Let \(\mathcal I\in\Inst_n(A,B)\) be arbitrary. Its group average under the normalised Haar measure on \(\mathrm U(B)\) and the uniform measure on \(S_n\) is

\[
\overline{\mathcal I}
:=
\int_G g\cdot \mathcal I\,dg.
\]

Averaging first over \(\mathrm U(B)\), Schur’s lemma gives for each component

\[
\int_{\mathrm U(B)}
U\, \mathcal I_k(X)\, U^\dagger\, dU
=
\frac{\Tr(\mathcal I_k(X))}{d_B}\, I_B.
\]

Averaging then over the outcome permutation group \(S_n\), the \(k\)-th component becomes

\[
\frac1n \sum_{j=1}^n
\frac{\Tr(\mathcal I_j(X))}{d_B}\, I_B
=
\frac{\Tr\bigl((\sum_j \mathcal I_j)(X)\bigr)}{n d_B}\, I_B
=
\frac{\Tr(X)}{n d_B}\, I_B,
\]

because \(\sum_j\mathcal I_j\) is trace preserving. Hence

\[
\overline{\mathcal I}
=
\mathcal I_*.
\]

#### Step 4: Concavity plus invariance yields optimality

Since \(f\) is concave and invariant,

\[
f(\mathcal I_*)
=
f\!\left(\int_G g\cdot\mathcal I\,dg\right)
\ge
\int_G f(g\cdot\mathcal I)\,dg
=
\int_G f(\mathcal I)\,dg
=
f(\mathcal I).
\]

Because \(\mathcal I\in\Inst_n(A,B)\) was arbitrary,

\[
f(\mathcal I_*)
=
\max_{\mathcal I_0\in\Inst_n(A,B)}
f(\mathcal I_0).
\]

Combining this with the exact value from Theorem 3.4 proves the claim.

Thus the uniform depolarising instrument is not merely a convenient centre; it is an optimal in-radius centre for the entire instrument body. The open question in Remark 3.4 is therefore closed.
