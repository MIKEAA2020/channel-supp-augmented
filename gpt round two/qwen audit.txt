## 1. Substantive proof gaps / mathematical issues

### 1.1. Major gap: the observable–replacement join is not justified by Proposition \ref{prop:join}

**Location.** Proposition \ref{prop:join}, Corollary \ref{cor:join-dimension}, Definition \ref{def:one-sphere}, Definition \ref{def:beta-best}, abstract.

**Issue.**  
Proposition \ref{prop:join} is stated for maps

\[
h_i:S^{r_i}\to \Sstate_{\mathrm{flag}}(B\otimes C_n)
\]

into flagged states supported on mutually orthogonal subspaces, and then composed with the replacement-instrument embedding. It therefore applies to replacement-instrument spheres only.

But the observable sphere from Theorem \ref{thm:observable} is not a replacement sphere. For \(n\ge 2\), the instrument constructed there has components

\[
\mathcal I^H_1(X)=\Tr(E_+(H)X)\sigma,\qquad
\mathcal I^H_2(X)=\Tr(E_-(H)X)\sigma,
\]

which are input-dependent. This is not of the replacement form

\[
X\mapsto \Tr(X)\tau_k.
\]

For \(n=1\), the channel

\[
\Phi^H(X)=\Tr(E_+(H)X)\sigma_+ + \Tr(E_-(H)X)\sigma_-
\]

is also not a replacement channel unless \(E_+(H),E_-(H)\) are independent of \(H\), which they are not.

Thus the sentence in Corollary \ref{cor:join-dimension},

> Proposition \ref{prop:join} joins the two spheres,

is not valid as written. The observable sphere is only “supported on \(W_{\mathrm{obs}}\)” at the level of the output support of its Choi operator; it is not a map into flagged states. Consequently the certified dimension \(L_{\mathrm{join}}\), and any value of \(L_{A,B}^{(n)}\) or \(\beta_r^{\mathrm{best},(n)}\) that relies on it, is not proved by the current text.

**Why this matters.**  
For some parameter values \(L_{\mathrm{join}}\) improves the certified one-sphere dimension. Example: Table row \((3,2,2)\) has

\[
L_{\mathrm{rep}}=6,\quad L_{\mathrm{obs}}=7,\quad L_{\mathrm{join}}=8.
\]

Without a valid join argument, the certified value would only be \(7\), not \(8\). The abstract and Definition \ref{def:beta-best} therefore overstate what is currently certified unless the join lemma is strengthened.

**Suggested fix.**  
Prove a stronger join lemma for instrument Choi operators with orthogonal output supports. A plausible statement is:

> Let \(h_1:S^{r_1}\to \Inst_n(A,B)\) be a continuous instrument sphere whose normalized flagged Choi operators are supported on \(A_0\otimes W_1\), and suppose
> \[
> \|h_1(u)-h_1(-u)\|_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}=2.
> \]
> Let \(h_2:S^{r_2}\to \RepInst_n(A,B)\) be a replacement sphere whose flagged states are supported on \(W_2\), with \(W_1\perp W_2\), and with antipodal distance \(2\). Then the convex join of the Choi operators gives a continuous map
> \[
> S^{r_1+r_2+1}\to \Inst_n(A,B)
> \]
> with antipodal instrument-diamond distance \(2\).

The proof would use that the replacement part attains distance \(2\) on every input state, while the observable part has some input witness attaining distance \(2\). For the joined point, choose the observable witness; the replacement part still contributes its full separation because it is input-independent. This should be stated explicitly. Without such a lemma, Corollary \ref{cor:join-dimension} is a gap.

### 1.2. Major gap: norm ambiguity in Proposition \ref{prop:convex}

**Location.** Proposition \ref{prop:convex}, proof.

**Issue.**  
The proof says:

> project affine coordinates ... Each nonempty fibre \(F\) ... has diameter at most two ... consider the family \(\{F\}\cup\{\overline B(x,R):x\in F\}\) in \(\aff(F)\simeq \mathbb R^k\).

But the diameter bound “at most two” is in the instrument diamond norm. The balls \(\overline B(x,R)\) and the barycentre estimates must be taken in the same norm. As written, “affine coordinates” and “balls” are not norm-specified. If one chooses arbitrary Euclidean affine coordinates, the Euclidean diameter of a fibre need not be \(2\), and the estimate

\[
\|c-x_j\|\le \frac{2(m-1)}m
\]

need not hold in the Euclidean coordinate norm.

**Suggested fix.**  
State explicitly that the affine hull is equipped with the instrument-diamond norm (or the norm transported from \(\mathfrak V_{A,B}^{(n)}\)). Then use norm balls, not necessarily Euclidean balls. Helly’s theorem applies to arbitrary compact convex sets in finite-dimensional real affine space, and norm balls are convex. The barycentre argument uses only the triangle inequality and the fact that the fibre diameter is \(\le 2\) in that norm.

Also clarify that the linear projection used as encoder is continuous in finite dimensions, and that decoder error is measured in the same norm.

This fix is likely enough, but it must be written explicitly.

### 1.3. Gap: continuity of constructed maps into the instrument diamond metric is often not stated

**Location.** Theorem \ref{thm:observable}, Theorem \ref{thm:full-direction}, partly Theorem \ref{thm:replacement-profile}.

**Issue.**  
The antipodal profile is defined using continuous maps into \(K\) with the instrument diamond metric. Some proofs only show continuity in Euclidean/operator/Choi norms.

Examples:

- In Theorem \ref{thm:observable}, the proof says the map \(H\mapsto H/\|H\|_\infty\) is continuous on the Euclidean unit sphere, but does not explicitly state that the resulting map into instruments is continuous in \(\|\cdot\|_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}\).

- In Theorem \ref{thm:full-direction}, continuity is argued for the Choi operators \(J^\pm(Z)\). One needs to say that continuity in Choi/operator norm implies continuity in the finite-dimensional instrument diamond norm.

- In Theorem \ref{thm:replacement-profile}, continuity is essentially in trace norm and then transferred by Lemma \ref{lem:replacement-isometry}; this is better, but still worth stating explicitly.

**Suggested fix.**  
Add a global sentence early in Section 4, for example:

> All constructions below are finite-dimensional. The normalized Choi representation identifies instruments with a compact convex subset of a finite-dimensional real vector space. Continuity in any norm on this space, in particular in Choi trace norm or operator norm, is equivalent to continuity in the instrument diamond norm.

Then refer to this whenever needed.

### 1.4. Ambiguity in the definition of \(N_{\mathrm{join}}\)

**Location.** Corollary \ref{cor:join-dimension}.

**Issue.**  
The text says:

> The orthogonal complement of \(W_{\mathrm{obs}}\) in the flag-block-diagonal Hermitian space has real dimension \(N_{\mathrm{join}}\).

This is ambiguous. There are at least two possible interpretations:

1. Hilbert-Schmidt orthogonal complement to the affine span of the observable flagged states.  
   This would have dimension \(nd_B^2-2\) in the \(n\ge 2\) case, not the stated formula.

2. Flag-block-diagonal Hermitian operators supported on the Hilbert-space orthogonal complement \(W_{\mathrm{obs}}^\perp\).  
   This gives the stated formula
   \[
   2(d_B-1)^2+(n-2)d_B^2.
   \]

The proof needs interpretation (2), but the wording invites interpretation (1).

**Suggested fix.**  
Rewrite as:

> Let \(\mathcal H_{\mathrm{comp}}\) be the real vector space of flag-block-diagonal Hermitian operators supported on the Hilbert-space orthogonal complement \(W_{\mathrm{obs}}^\perp\). Its real dimension is ...

Then the formula is unambiguous.

### 1.5. Reference dimension not explicitly restricted in the covering-radius upper bound

**Location.** Theorem \ref{thm:covering}, upper-bound proof.

**Issue.**  
The proof fixes a “reference-assisted input state” and then uses

\[
\rank \rho_{k,E}\le d_A.
\]

This is true only if the reference system \(E\) has dimension at most \(d_A\). The diamond norm can indeed be restricted to a reference of input dimension \(d_A\), and this is stated earlier in the paper, but at this point the proof should explicitly say:

> By the standard reduction for the diamond norm, we may take \(\dim E=d_A\).

Otherwise the rank bound is not justified for an arbitrary reference.

**Suggested fix.**  
Insert a sentence at the beginning of the upper-bound proof:

> Since the diamond norm of a map with input \(A\) is attained with a reference of dimension \(d_A\), we take \(\dim E=d_A\).

---

### 1.6. Missing explicit ranges for pinching parameters in Definition \ref{def:upper}

**Location.** Definition \ref{def:upper}.

**Issue.**  
The definition minimizes over “all output-pinching values” and “all input-pinching values” satisfying certain inequalities, but it does not explicitly restrict

\[
1\le \ell_{\mathrm{out}}\le d_B,\qquad 1\le k_{\mathrm{in}}\le d_A.
\]

The quantity \(Q_\ell(d)\) was defined only for \(1\le \ell\le d\), and physical block decompositions require positive block sizes.

**Suggested fix.**  
Add:

> Here \(\ell_{\mathrm{out}}\) ranges over \(1,\dots,d_B\), and \(k_{\mathrm{in}}\) ranges over \(1,\dots,d_A\). Families outside these ranges are ignored.

---

### 1.7. Degenerate singleton case not consistently excluded in summary table

**Location.** Table \ref{tab:comparison}, “Channel \(n=1\)” column.

**Issue.**  
The table gives for channels:

\[
\text{Centred in-radius } \frac{2}{d_B s},
\qquad
\text{Replacement sphere } S^{d_B^2-2}.
\]

But for \(n=1,d_B=1\), the body is the degenerate singleton trace functional, the centred radius is \(+\infty\) under the paper’s convention, and the replacement sphere would be \(S^{-1}\). The table does not explicitly exclude this case.

**Suggested fix.**  
Add a note to the table:

> The channel column assumes the nondegenerate case \(d_B>1\); the case \(n=d_B=1\) is the singleton discussed in Remark \ref{rem:degenerate}.

---

### 1.8. Remark on covering-number encodings is heuristic and mixes bits with continuous dimension

**Location.** Remark \ref{rem:barrier}, paragraph on covering-number encodings.

**Issue.**  
The remark says an \(\varepsilon\)-net needs \(\Theta(D\log_2(1/\varepsilon))\) bits and is therefore dominated by affine coordinates. But the compression width in the paper is a continuous latent dimension, not a discrete bit count. A continuous encoder into \(\mathbb R^r\) is not directly constrained by bit-counting. The argument is suggestive but not a rigorous obstruction in the paper’s model.

**Suggested fix.**  
Either remove this paragraph or qualify it clearly:

> This is only a heuristic comparison with discrete net-based codes; it is not used in the proofs and does not by itself rule out continuous low-dimensional encodings.

## 4. Summary of required corrections

The most important fixes are:

1. **Fix or remove the observable–replacement join.**  
   Proposition \ref{prop:join} does not apply to Theorem \ref{thm:observable}. Either prove a stronger join lemma for instrument spheres with orthogonal output supports, or remove \(L_{\mathrm{join}}\) from the certified envelope.

2. **Specify the norm in Proposition \ref{prop:convex}.**  
   The Helly/barycentre argument must be performed in the instrument diamond norm or a transported version of it.

3. **State finite-dimensional continuity explicitly.**  
   All antipodal constructions should be explicitly continuous in the instrument diamond metric.

4. **Clarify \(N_{\mathrm{join}}\).**  
   Define it as the dimension of flag-block-diagonal Hermitian operators supported on \(W_{\mathrm{obs}}^\perp\).

5. **Fix forward references.**  
   Especially Proposition \ref{prop:diameter} using Lemma \ref{lem:replacement-isometry} early, and Corollary \ref{cor:bracket} using \(U_r^{(n)}\) before definition.

6. **Add small missing hypotheses/ranges.**  
   Reference dimension in Theorem \ref{thm:covering}; pinching parameter ranges in Definition \ref{def:upper}; degenerate-case exclusion in Table \ref{tab:comparison}.

7. **Qualify the covering-number remark.**  
   It is heuristic and not part of the rigorous barrier.

With these corrections, the main exact geometry appears sound. The join issue is the only one that currently affects the quantitative certified lower envelope beyond the replacement and observable dimensions.
