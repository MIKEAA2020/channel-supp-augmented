# A. Remaining mathematical issues

## A.1. The observable construction for \(n=1\) has a missing case in its eigenvector argument

In the \(n\ge2\) proof you write:

> “If \(K(H)|\psi\rangle=|\psi\rangle\), then … for \(-H\) the roles of \(E_+\) and \(E_-\) are exchanged.”

But \(\|K(H)\|_\infty=1\) only guarantees an eigenvalue \(+1\) **or** \(-1\). If the selected eigenvalue is \(-1\), then for \(H\) the roles are already reversed.

This is not a serious mathematical problem, but the proof should handle both cases explicitly.

### Replace the relevant paragraph by

```latex
Since \(\|K(H)\|_\infty=1\), \(K(H)\) has a unit eigenvector
\(|\psi\rangle\) with eigenvalue \(\varepsilon\in\{+1,-1\}\). Then
\[
\Tr(E_{\varepsilon}(H)|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|)=1,
\qquad
\Tr(E_{-\varepsilon}(H)|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|)=0,
\]
where \(E_{+1}(H)=E_+(H)\) and \(E_{-1}(H)=E_-(H)\).
For \(-H\), the two effects are exchanged:
\[
E_+(-H)=E_-(H),\qquad E_-(-H)=E_+(H).
\]
Therefore the flagged outputs of \(\mathcal I^H\) and
\(\mathcal I^{-H}\) are supported on different classical flags and are
orthogonal.
```

The same correction should be made in the \(n=1\) paragraph.

## A.2. The “observable sphere” is a continuous image, not necessarily a sphere in the body

The theorem currently says:

> “Then \(\alpha_r(\Inst_n(A,B))=1\)…”

This conclusion is correct, but the phrase “observable antipodal sphere” can suggest an embedding. The map
\[
H\longmapsto \mathcal I^H
\]
need not be injective because \(H\) and positive scalar multiples of \(H\) are identified by the normalization \(H/\|H\|_\infty\), and additional degeneracies may occur.

You already make this distinction correctly in the replacement theorem. Make it explicit here too.

### Suggested theorem wording

```latex
Then there exists a continuous map
\[
h:S^{d_A^2-2}\longrightarrow\Inst_n(A,B)
\]
whose antipodal images have instrument-diamond distance \(2\).
Consequently,
\[
\alpha_r(\Inst_n(A,B))=1
\qquad(0\le r\le d_A^2-2).
\]
No injectivity of \(h\) is asserted.
```

## A.3. The full-direction construction uses a notation clash between \(P\) and projectors

In Theorem~\ref{thm:full-direction}, you define
\[
P=\frac{Z_+}{t},\qquad Q=\frac{Z_-}{t}.
\]
Earlier, and elsewhere in the paper, \(P\) is used for orthogonal projections. This is not merely cosmetic in a Choi-operator proof.

### Replace

\[
P=\frac{Z_+}{t},\qquad Q=\frac{Z_-}{t}
\]

by

\[
\Theta_+=\frac{Z_+}{t},\qquad
\Theta_-=\frac{Z_-}{t}.
\]

Then use \(\Theta_\pm\) consistently:
\[
J^+(Z)=\lambda\Theta_++(1-\lambda)\nu\otimes\tau_0,
\]
\[
J^-(Z)=\lambda\Theta_-+(1-\lambda)\nu\otimes\tau_0.
\]

This avoids confusion with the projection \(P_W\).

## A.4. The full-direction theorem should explicitly justify that \(J^\pm(Z)\) are block diagonal in the outcome flag

You state:

> “Both \(J^\pm(Z)\) are positive and flag-block diagonal…”

The first summands are flag-block diagonal because \(Z\) is. The filler term is flag-block diagonal because
\[
\tau_0=\frac{I_B}{d_B}\otimes\frac{I_{C_n}}n
\]
is diagonal in the flag basis. This is straightforward but should be stated.

### Add

```latex
Because \(Z\) is flag-block diagonal, so are \(Z_\pm\), hence
\(\Theta_\pm\). The filler \(\nu\otimes\tau_0\) is also flag-block
diagonal because \(\tau_0\) is diagonal in the fixed flag basis.
```

## A.5. The full-direction theorem has a hidden \(D=0\) issue

The theorem assumes \(nd_B>1\), so
\[
D=d_A^2(nd_B^2-1)>0.
\]
Thus \(S^{D-1}\) is meaningful. This is fine.

However, the table and several specialisations state the full-direction sphere formula without repeating the nontriviality condition. In particular, for the singleton case \(n=d_B=1\),
\[
D=0,
\]
and \(S^{-1}\) is not being used in the usual topological sense.

### Required correction

Qualify the table row and every specialisation by:

```latex
For \(nd_B>1\), the full-direction construction has domain
\(S^{D_{A,B}^{(n)}-1}\).
```

Do not present \(S^{-1}\) as an ordinary sphere in the degenerate case.

## A.6. The replacement profile theorem should state explicitly why \(\alpha_r=0\) for \(r\ge m\)

The proof is correct, but one sentence is slightly imprecise:

> “compose any map \(S^r\to\RepInst_n(A,B)\) with the inverse isometry and affine coordinates in \(\mathbb R^m\).”

The inverse isometry maps instruments to the compact convex state body, but “affine coordinates” should be identified with an affine isomorphism from the trace-one hyperplane to \(\mathbb R^m\).

### Replace by

```latex
The replacement family is affinely isometric, under
Lemma~\ref{lem:replacement-isometry}, to the compact convex body
\[
K_{\mathrm{rep}}
=\{\tau\in\Sstate_{\mathrm{flag}}(B\otimes C_n)\}
\]
inside a real affine space of dimension \(m=nd_B^2-1\).
Choose an affine isomorphism from its affine hull to \(\mathbb R^m\).
For any continuous \(h:S^r\to K_{\mathrm{rep}}\), restrict to an
equatorial \(S^m\subseteq S^r\) when \(r>m\), and apply
Borsuk--Ulam to the resulting map \(S^m\to\mathbb R^m\).
```

## A.7. The convex-projection bound needs a precise norm notation

In Proposition~\ref{prop:convex}, you write
\[
\overline B(x,R)
\]
without specifying whether this is Euclidean or instrument-diamond. The proof uses the instrument-diamond norm, because the fibre diameter is bounded by \(2\) in that norm.

### Replace

\[
\overline B(x,R)
\]

by

\[
\overline B_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}(x,R)
=
\{z:\|z-x\|_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}\le R\}.
\]

Then state:

```latex
These are convex closed sets in the finite-dimensional affine space
\(\aff(F)\), because every norm ball is convex.
```

The barycentre estimate should also be written as
\[
\|c-x_j\|_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}
\le \frac{2(m-1)}m.
\]

## A.8. The convex-projection proof has a small \(k=0\) issue

You state the proposition for

The proof says:

> “Otherwise project … and put \(k=D-r\).”

For \(r=D\), \(k=0\), and the preceding “otherwise” excludes that case. The statement is correct, but the proof should separately handle \(r=D\) before assuming \(k\ge1\).

### Replace the opening by

```latex
If \(r=D_{A,B}^{(n)}\), affine coordinates give exact reconstruction.
Assume henceforth \(0\le r<D_{A,B}^{(n)}\), and put
\[
k=D_{A,B}^{(n)}-r\ge1.
\]
```

## A.9. The output pinching Choi formula should be displayed

The proof says:

> “the reconstruction of \(\mathcal I\) is \((\mathcal P_B\otimes\id_{C_n})\circ\mathcal I\)”

This is type-ambiguous: \(\mathcal P_B\otimes\id_{C_n}\) acts on the output of the flagged channel, not on the tuple directly.

### Replace by

```latex
Define the reconstructed instrument by
\[
\widetilde{\mathcal I}_k=\mathcal P_B\circ\mathcal I_k,
\qquad k=1,\dots,n.
\]
Equivalently, its flagged channel is
\[
\widehat{\widetilde{\mathcal I}}
=(\mathcal P_B\otimes\id_{C_n})\circ\widehat{\mathcal I}.
\]
```

Then the norm estimate is unambiguous.

## A.10. The input pinching Choi identity contains a notation error

You write:
\[
\Jhat_{\Phi\circ\mathcal P_A}
=(\mathcal P_{\overline A_0}\otimes\id_B)\Jhat_\Phi.
\]

There is no previously defined space \(\overline A_0\). The intended object is the conjugate block-pinching channel on \(A_0\), which should be denoted, for example,
\[
\mathcal P_{\overline A_0}
=
\sum_i P_{\overline{A_i}}(\cdot)P_{\overline{A_i}}.
\]

### Add before the formula

```latex
Let
\[
\mathcal P_{\overline A_0}(Y)
=\sum_iP_{\overline{A_i}}YP_{\overline{A_i}}
\]
be the block pinching on the conjugate reference space \(A_0\).
With the fixed Choi bases,
\[
\Jhat_{\Phi\circ\mathcal P_A}
=
(\mathcal P_{\overline A_0}\otimes\id_B)(\Jhat_\Phi).
\]
```

The present notation is also inconsistent with the earlier phrase “use the basis-conjugate decomposition \(A_0=\bigoplus_i\overline A_i\).”

## A.11. The input/output pinching bounds need the range condition \(r\le D\) handled explicitly

The theorem states bounds “for \(r\ge r_{\mathrm{out}}^{(n)}\)” and “for \(r\ge r_{\mathrm{in}}^{(n)}\).” If \(r>D\), the width is already zero, so the inequalities remain true, but the coordinate encoding argument only needs to be given for
\[
r_{\mathrm{out}}^{(n)}\le r<D
\]
and similarly for input pinching.

### Suggested formulation

```latex
For every \(r\ge r_{\mathrm{out}}^{(n)}\), the bound holds; for
\(r\ge D_{A,B}^{(n)}\) it is understood through the exact
zero-error theorem.
```

This avoids suggesting that one embeds an affine body of dimension \(r_{\mathrm{out}}\) into a latent space of dimension \(r\) without explaining padding.

# B. One substantive claim should be weakened

## B.1. “The affine encoding already achieves error \(0\) with exactly \(D\) coordinates”

In Remark~\ref{rem:barrier}, you write:

> “The affine encoding already achieves error \(0\) with exactly \(D\) coordinates…”

This is true under your convention, but the phrase “exactly \(D\) coordinates” can be misread as asserting that \(D\) is a minimal coordinate count for arbitrary, possibly discontinuous, encoders. The zero-error theorem proves minimality for **continuous encoders into Euclidean spaces**, not for arbitrary codes.

### Replace by

```latex
The affine encoding achieves error \(0\) with \(D\) Euclidean

also necessary when the encoder is required to be continuous.
```

# C. The no-programming appendix contains one important technical issue

## C.1. The definition of \(\Lambda_j\) in the approximate-program proof is not dimensionally transparent

You define
\[
\Lambda_j(\eta)=W_j^\dagger P_j\eta P_jW_j
+
\Tr[(I_M-P_j)\eta]\tau_j.
\]

This is correct if \(\tau_j\) is regarded as a state on \(M_j\). But the first term has output in \(M_j\), while the second term is written without explicitly indicating the embedding. It would be clearer to define
\[
\Lambda_j:\Lin(M)\to\Lin(M_j)
\]
and write
\[
\Lambda_j(\eta)
=
W_j^\dagger P_j\eta P_jW_j
+
\Tr[(I_M-P_j)\eta]\,\tau_j.
\]

Then state that this map is CPTP because it is the sum of:
- a compression channel on the \(P_j\)-corner;
- a measure-and-prepare channel on the orthogonal complement.

Also, the first term should technically be written
\[
W_j^\dagger P_j\eta P_jW_j
=
W_j^\dagger\eta W_j,
\]
since \(P_jW_j=W_j\), but retaining \(P_j\) makes the trace decomposition visible.

# G. The net argument in Remark~\ref{rem:barrier} is too informal and should either be removed or weakened

The passage

> “An \(\varepsilon\)-net of a \(D\)-dimensional convex body has size \(N(\varepsilon)\ge(r_0/\varepsilon)^D\) up to constants…”

requires hypotheses and a precise metric-volume argument. In particular:

- the body must contain a norm ball of radius \(r_0\);
- the covering metric must be the same norm, or norm-equivalence constants must be included;
- “up to constants” is not enough for the subsequent phrase “strictly dominated”;
- a net-based code is not automatically subject to the same continuity constraint.

Since this remark is not used in any theorem, the safest action is to replace it with a qualitative statement.

### Suggested replacement

```latex
A finite \(\varepsilon\)-net gives a discontinuous, finite-code
approximation scheme, but it does not improve the present continuous
Euclidean-coordinate results. Quantitative covering-number estimates
would require a separate choice of metric-volume normalization and are
not needed here.
```

# I. A better formulation of the global covering-radius proof

The proof is correct, but one sentence should be made more precise:

> “The functional \(R_{\mathrm{inst}}\) is convex, being a supremum of norms.”

For fixed \(\mathcal I\),
\[
\Psi\mapsto\|\mathcal I-\Psi\|_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}
\]
is convex, and a supremum of convex functions is convex.

### Replace by

```latex
For each fixed \(\mathcal I\), the function
\[
\Psi\longmapsto
\|\mathcal I-\Psi\|_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}
\]
is convex. Hence its supremum over \(\mathcal I\in\Inst_n(A,B)\),
namely \(R_{\mathrm{inst}}\), is convex.
```

This avoids any concern that “a supremum of norms” is being invoked without identifying the variable.

# O. Overall assessment after revision

After the above corrections, the mathematical structure is coherent:

1. The affine dimension proof is sound.
2. The spectral estimate and centred-radius theorem are sound.
3. The covering-radius argument is sound once the group action and convexity are written in full.
4. The replacement isometry and exact replacement profile are sound.
5. The full-direction construction is valid and supplies the \(1/d_A\) lower bound.
6. The observable construction is valid, with the \(\pm1\) eigenvalue case made explicit.
7. The join construction is valid.
8. The Borsuk--Ulam zero-error threshold is valid under continuous encoding and unrestricted decoding, and the continuous decoder at the threshold is correctly supplied by metric projection.
9. The pinching bounds are valid after correcting the Choi-space notation.
10. The no-programming appendix is logically independent and broadly correct.

The most important remaining fixes are:

- precise norm notation in the convex-projection argument;
- correction of the input-pinching Choi formula;
- explicit handling of both eigenvalue signs in the observable construction;
- removal of the net-counting overstatement;
- qualification of sphere formulas in singleton cases;
- replacing ambiguous “sphere” language by “continuous antipodally separated image” where injectivity is not known.

With these changes, the manuscript’s claims would be appropriately scoped and the main theorem package would be rigorous.
