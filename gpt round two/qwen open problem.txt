the open question in Remark \ref{rem:global-inradius} can be settled affirmatively by the same symmetry/averaging method already used for the covering radius. The key point is that any relative diamond ball contained in the instrument body can be averaged over the output-unitary and outcome-permutation group; its centre averages to \(\mathcal I_*\), while the ball itself is invariant. Thus no other centre can have a larger relative in-radius.

Below is a proof written in the paper’s notation.

---

## Proposed proposition

Assume \(nd_B>1\). Let

\[
s=\min\{d_A,d_B\}.
\]

Then

\[
\sup_{\mathcal I_0\in \Inst_n(A,B)}
\rho_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}(\mathcal I_0)
=
\rho_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}(\mathcal I_*)
=
\frac{2}{n d_B s}.
\]

In particular, the uniform depolarising instrument \(\mathcal I_*\) is an optimal in-radius centre.

The degenerate case \(n=d_B=1\) is trivial: the body is a singleton, the direction space is \(\{0\}\), and the in-radius is \(+\infty\) under the paper’s supremum convention.

---

## Proof

Let

\[
K=\Inst_n(A,B),
\qquad
V=\mathfrak V_{A,B}^{(n)},
\]

and let

\[
B_{\diamond}
=
\{\Delta\in V:\|\Delta\|_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}\le 1\}
\]

be the closed unit ball in the direction space.

For \(\mathcal I_0\in K\), the centred relative in-radius is

\[
\rho_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}(\mathcal I_0)
=
\sup\{r\ge 0:\mathcal I_0+rB_{\diamond}\subseteq K\}.
\]

We will show that for every \(\mathcal I_0\in K\),

\[
\rho_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}(\mathcal I_0)
\le
\rho_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}(\mathcal I_*).
\]

---

### 1. The symmetry group

Let

\[
G=\mathrm U(B)\times S_n
\]

act on instruments by output unitary conjugation and outcome permutation:

\[
[(U,\pi)\cdot \mathcal J]_k
=
\mathcal U_U\circ \mathcal J_{\pi^{-1}(k)},
\qquad
\mathcal U_U(X)=UXU^\dagger.
\]

This action has the following properties.

1. **It preserves the instrument body.**  
   If \(\mathcal J\in \Inst_n(A,B)\), then \((U,\pi)\cdot \mathcal J\in \Inst_n(A,B)\).

2. **It preserves the instrument diamond norm.**  
   On flagged maps, the action is conjugation by a unitary on \(B\otimes C_n\), followed by a permutation of the classical flag. Hence for every \(\Delta\in V\),

   \[
   \|(U,\pi)\cdot \Delta\|_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}
   =
   \|\Delta\|_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}.
   \]

   Therefore \(G\) preserves \(B_{\diamond}\).

3. **The barycentre of every instrument orbit is \(\mathcal I_*\).**  
   Let \(\mathcal J=(\mathcal J_1,\dots,\mathcal J_n)\in K\). Average first over \(\mathrm U(B)\). For each component,

   \[
   \int_{\mathrm U(B)}
   U\mathcal J_j(X)U^\dagger\,dU
   =
   \frac{\Tr(\mathcal J_j(X))}{d_B}I_B.
   \]

   Then average over outcome permutations. The \(k\)-th component becomes

   \[
   \frac1n\sum_{j=1}^n
   \frac{\Tr(\mathcal J_j(X))}{d_B}I_B
   =
   \frac{\Tr(X)}{n d_B}I_B,
   \]

   because \(\sum_j\mathcal J_j\) is trace preserving. This is exactly

   \[
   (\mathcal I_*)_k(X)=\frac{\Tr(X)}{n d_B}I_B.
   \]

   Hence

   \[
   \int_G g\mathcal J\,d\mu(g)=\mathcal I_*,
   \]

   where \(\mu\) is normalized Haar measure on \(G\).

---

### 2. Averaging transfers any contained ball to \(\mathcal I_*\)

Fix \(\mathcal I_0\in K\). Suppose

\[
\mathcal I_0+rB_{\diamond}\subseteq K
\]

for some \(r\ge 0\).

For every \(g\in G\), since \(gK=K\) and \(gB_{\diamond}=B_{\diamond}\),

\[
g\mathcal I_0+rB_{\diamond}
=
g(\mathcal I_0+rB_{\diamond})
\subseteq K.
\]

Now fix an arbitrary direction \(\Delta\in B_{\diamond}\). For every \(g\in G\),

\[
g\mathcal I_0+r\Delta\in K.
\]

Averaging these points over \(G\), and using convexity and closedness of \(K\), gives

\[
\int_G (g\mathcal I_0+r\Delta)\,d\mu(g)
=
\left(\int_G g\mathcal I_0\,d\mu(g)\right)+r\Delta
=
\mathcal I_*+r\Delta
\in K.
\]

Since \(\Delta\in B_{\diamond}\) was arbitrary,

\[
\mathcal I_*+rB_{\diamond}\subseteq K.
\]

Therefore

\[
r\le \rho_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}(\mathcal I_*).
\]

Taking the supremum over all \(r\) such that \(\mathcal I_0+rB_{\diamond}\subseteq K\), we obtain

\[
\rho_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}(\mathcal I_0)
\le
\rho_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}(\mathcal I_*).
\]

Since \(\mathcal I_0\in K\) was arbitrary, \(\mathcal I_*\) maximises the centred relative in-radius over the whole instrument body.

---

### 3. Insert the exact centred value

By Theorem \ref{thm:exact-radius},

\[
\rho_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}(\mathcal I_*)
=
\frac{2}{n d_B\min\{d_A,d_B\}}.
\]

Hence

\[
\sup_{\mathcal I_0\in \Inst_n(A,B)}
\rho_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}(\mathcal I_0)
=
\frac{2}{n d_B\min\{d_A,d_B\}},
\]

and \(\mathcal I_*\) is an optimal centre.

---

## Suggested replacement for Remark \ref{rem:global-inradius}

You could replace the current remark with something like:

> **Remark [Global in-radius].**  
> The centred radius of Theorem \ref{thm:exact-radius} is also the global in-radius. Let \(B_{\diamond}\) be the unit ball in the direction space. If \(\mathcal I_0+rB_{\diamond}\subseteq \Inst_n(A,B)\), then applying the output-unitary/outcome-permutation group \(G=\mathrm U(B)\times S_n\) gives \(g\mathcal I_0+rB_{\diamond}\subseteq \Inst_n(A,B)\) for every \(g\in G\). Averaging pointwise over \(G\), and using that the barycentre of every orbit is \(\mathcal I_*\), gives \(\mathcal I_*+rB_{\diamond}\subseteq \Inst_n(A,B)\). Hence no centre can have a larger relative in-radius than \(\mathcal I_*\). Therefore
> \[
> \sup_{\mathcal I_0\in\Inst_n(A,B)}
> \rho_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}(\mathcal I_0)
> =
> \rho_{\diamond,\mathrm{inst}}(\mathcal I_*)
> =
> \frac{2}{n d_B\min\{d_A,d_B\}}.
> \]

This also means that the item in Remark \ref{rem:exact-vs-certified} listing “the global in-radius over all centres” as open can be upgraded to an exact determination.
